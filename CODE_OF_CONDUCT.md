# Code of Conduct

## Our standard

We want this project to be a useful, rigorous place to discuss and improve agent skills. Treat other participants with respect. Critique claims, evidence and changes—not people.

## Expected behavior

- Be constructive, specific and honest about uncertainty.
- Respect different backgrounds, experience levels and good-faith disagreement.
- Keep discussion relevant to the project and its contributors.
- Protect privacy: do not post personal data, secrets, customer material, private conversations or private evaluation data.
- Respect intellectual-property and attribution boundaries.

## Unacceptable behavior

- Harassment, threats, hate speech, discrimination, sexualized language or unwanted contact.
- Personal attacks, deliberate intimidation, doxxing, impersonation or publishing private information.
- Spam, manipulation of discussion, knowingly false technical claims, or attempts to introduce credentials, malware, exploit payloads or harmful instructions.
- Posting copyrighted material that you do not have permission to share.

## Scope

This code applies to repository issues, pull requests, discussions, reviews and other project spaces. It also applies when someone represents the project in a public setting.

## Reporting and enforcement

Do not report an incident by publishing sensitive details. Use GitHub private reporting when it is enabled; otherwise, open a minimal issue requesting a private reporting channel without naming people or including evidence publicly.

Maintainers may remove content, edit or close discussions, reject contributions, restrict participation, or report serious abuse to GitHub. They will assess reports in good faith, protect privacy where possible, and explain an action when doing so does not create additional harm or expose private information.

Retaliation against someone who raises a good-faith concern is not accepted.
