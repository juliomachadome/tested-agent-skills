# Creative playbooks: routines and templates

Eight routines. Budget, kill and scale numbers belong to paid-traffic; law detail to `policy-and-law.md`; reading results to `diagnosis-playbook.md`. Evidence tags as in `SKILL.md`. Price, deadline and guarantee text comes from the offer owner (offers), final wording from copywriting.

## 1. Creative brief (one page, per concept)
Fill every line; a blank line is a question to the requester.
```
Concept line: for [persona] who [situation], show [proof] in [format], opening with [hook], asking for [action]
Awareness stage (cold social usually unaware/problem-aware) and market sophistication:
Single idea (fits on a poster):
Proof on screen (real product in use, real count, demonstration):
Hook (first 3 s): line + frame 0 description. Does frame 0 work as a still?
Body beats (3-5), each with the seconds it ends at:
CTA: on screen + in voice; label equals the platform button; destination URL
Destination first screen repeats: image / phrase / offer (yes/no)
Allowed claims, price, deadline, guarantee: source (offer owner / page) and expiry date
Do not say (policy and law flags): personal attributes, outcome promises, before/after, urgency
Language, country, register; faithful translation needed for approver? Jokes flagged for native check
Formats and placements; safe zone checked in preview
Assets: real screens, own footage; licences logged; people: who, release on file
AI use: none / voice / background; disclosure decision and who made it
Tracking ID = file name; expiry in file name if any
Hypothesis: "if [change], then [metric] moves because [reason]"; kill and keep rule (from paid-traffic)
```

## 2. Concept sprint (10 concepts in one session)
Goal: ten different one-line concepts, then 3-6 into the batch.
1. Fill the 5-line diagnosis (`SKILL.md` §1) once.
2. Grid: rows = 5 angles (pain, desire/result, demo/how it works, social proof/comparison, myth or objection); columns = awareness stage (unaware/problem-aware, solution-aware, product-aware). Write one concept line in at least 10 cells, not adjacent copies. (Awareness levels follow Schwartz, *Breakthrough Advertising*, **[M]** practitioner framework, single book.)
3. Per concept add proof type (screen, hand, person, number) and format (UGC, screen recording, motion text, static, carousel).
4. Dedupe: two lines with the same persona, proof and angle are one concept; merge.
5. Score each 1-3 on: real proof available now, can be produced in the week, clear differentiation from other lines, policy risk (low = 3). Pick the top 3-6, keeping at least two stages and two formats.
6. Output: ranked table, the chosen set with hypotheses, the dropped ones with the reason (kept for later rounds).
Time box: 60-90 minutes. Do not write scripts for all ten.

## 3. UGC creator brief and rights checklist
**Brief (give beats, not a script)**
- Product, one-sentence promise, the one thing the creator must show (real use), the one thing they must not say (claims list, medical/financial/outcome promises, personal attributes).
- Situation to act out (their own words), 3-5 beats with target seconds, frame 0 request, one CTA line, deliverables (9:16, 1080×1920, length, raw file, 2-3 alternative hooks, captions file, clean audio), delivery date, revisions (1 round).
- Disclosure: creator discloses the paid relationship in the post or ad per platform tool and law; free product counts as a material connection (FTC Endorsement Guides 16 CFR 255.5, 2023 revision, read 15/11/2026 **[C]**; FTC *Disclosures 101* page dated 2019 says in-video disclosure, not only in the description, and warns against burying or vague terms **[C]**). In the EU, unmarked paid content is a blacklisted practice (UCPD Annex I item 11, advertorial, text read 15/11/2026).
- Authenticity: the creator says only what is true about their use; no invented experience; no reading claims the advertiser cannot substantiate (FTC 255.2 consumer endorsements need substantiation **[C]**).

**Usage rights and whitelisting checklist** (contract terms are for counsel; this is a prompt list, not legal advice)
- [ ] Written agreement before shooting: scope (paid ads, organic, website), platforms, countries, term (start and end dates), exclusivity, fee, payment date.
- [ ] Licence of the footage and of the creator's name, likeness and voice for those uses and that term; what happens at expiry (stop and take down).
- [ ] Whitelisting / partnership ads (the ad runs from the creator's handle): creator grants access through the platform's own tool; duration, spend cap, revocation, who owns comments and metrics.
- [ ] Right to edit, cut, add captions, translate; no AI cloning of voice or face unless expressly agreed in writing.
- [ ] Music: creator uses licensed or platform-cleared audio for ads; trending sounds from the organic library often are not cleared for ads **[F]**; log the licence.
- [ ] Third-party content in frame: logos, other brands, people, art, children (parental consent) cleared or removed.
- [ ] Data: GDPR consent and a contact for the creator; personal data minimal; retention date.
- [ ] Records: contract, release, licence terms, disclosure wording, file names, takedown date in a credits file.

## 4. Test batch design (handoff to paid-traffic)
1. Question first: "which concept sells" (first batch, mix concepts) or "why/how much" (iterating a winner, change one thing).
2. Size by budget: ask paid-traffic for the per-creative spend rule (≈2.5× CPA per ad per period in its playbook) and divide: ads that fit = period budget ÷ (2.5 × target CPA). Example: 600 per week, target CPA 25 → 600 ÷ 62.5 = 9.6, so at most 9, but run 3-6 concepts because distinct ones matter more than count **[F]**. With a small budget, 3 concepts in 2 formats beat 12 hook swaps **[C platform guidance on diverse sets; practitioner for exact counts]**.
3. Structure: one ad group, 3-5 distinct creatives (TikTok guidance), same audience, placements, objective and optimisation event; new creatives join existing ads group instead of opening new ones.
4. Pre-register before launch (Kohavi et al.): primary metric (purchases/CPA or revenue per visitor), guardrails (refund rate, visit ÷ click), decision date and rule; do not change the winner rule after seeing data **[M]**.
5. Handoff package to paid-traffic: concept lines, file names = tracking IDs, destination URLs, expiry dates, desired budget per ad, decision date, what counts as a win (e.g. CPA at or below target with at least 10 purchases, or repeat the result in another week).
6. Hook/hold are for early triage after ~5,000 impressions per ad; purchases decide. Say "not enough volume to conclude" when true.
7. Result: batch report (`diagnosis-playbook.md`, last section) with decisions scale / iterate / kill and the next hypothesis.

## 5. Winner iteration ladder (one change at a time)
Climb only after the concept won on purchases with volume. Each rung is one change; keep the others fixed and give it a tracking ID.
| Rung | Change | Question it answers | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | New 3-s hook (3 variants), same body | Is the stop the limit? | low |
| 2 | Hook type swap (question, number, situation, demo) | Which opening type for this audience | low |
| 3 | Format swap: screen recording ↔ hand with product ↔ talking head ↔ motion text ↔ static | Does the format carry the result **[F, 1-SRC]** | medium |
| 4 | Length: 6-10 s cut vs 20-30 s vs 45+ s | Is hold the limit? | low (re-edit) |
| 5 | Proof swap (another demo, another number, a real review) | Is trust the limit? | medium |
| 6 | CTA and end card wording; destination first screen | Is the click or page the limit? | low |
| 7 | New creator or voice | Is the person the limit? | high |
| 8 | New angle on the same product | The concept is exhausted; start a new sprint | high |
Stop climbing when three consecutive rungs give no gain beyond noise; move to rung 8. A hook swap on a fatigued body does not cure fatigue.

## 6. Weekly creative review (45 minutes, same day each week)
1. Data hygiene (5 min): `diagnosis-playbook.md` Step 0 for the week.
2. Scoreboard (10 min): table of every live creative by concept: spend, hook, hold, CTR, visits, purchases, CPA, revenue per visitor, frequency, age in days; decisions per row.
3. Winners and why (5 min): one-line reason tied to a symptom row.
4. Losers and cause (5 min): kill, iterate, or "no volume".
5. Fatigue watch (5 min): frequency, CTR trend, share of spend in the top ad; list ads older than their refresh age.
6. Policy and law (5 min): rejections, comments, expiring prices or dates, licences expiring.
7. Next batch (10 min): approve concepts and hypotheses; assign owners and dates; hand the budget request to paid-traffic.
Output: one page with decisions and next hypotheses. Update agent memory with what sold, failed, was vetoed.

## 7. Refresh calendar
Starting points **[F]**; replace with your own fatigue history after 6 weeks.
| Cadence | Action |
|---|---|
| Weekly | Review (§6); 1 new hook on the best body if rung 1 is cheap |
| Every 2-3 weeks | 1-2 new concepts into the live group; retire the weakest by purchases and fatigue signal |
| Monthly | Concept sprint (§2) if fewer than 3 distinct concepts are healthy; refresh proof (new screens, new numbers) |
| Quarterly | Audit the whole library: concepts exhausted, formats untested, creators' licence terms, price/date claims, policy changes |
| Before a promo or date | Build expiring creatives with the date in the file name; schedule takedown the same day |
Small audiences tire faster and need new concepts sooner; check reach growth against spend.

## 8. AI-generated faces and voices: disclosure checklist
Not legal advice; rules change. Dates and obligations to re-check on the day.
- [ ] Inventory: what in the piece is AI-generated or AI-altered (face, body, voice, background, music, script text). Product, screens and results are never AI-generated; no AI person presented as a customer, expert or real person.
- [ ] Synthetic voice used as unnamed narration, not as a named person; no voice clone of a real person without written consent.
- [ ] Platform rules: TikTok's ad policy permits significantly AI-generated or AI-altered media only with an AI-generated content label or clear disclaimer, caption, watermark or sticker (misleading-content policy, read 15/11/2026); Meta labels content as "AI info" through detection or self-disclosure (Meta newsroom, 2024; ad-specific rules were not read; verify in the ads manager); YouTube/Google: check the current disclosure setting in the ad flow (not read this pass).
- [ ] EU AI Act art. 50 (Regulation 2024/1689): the original text, read 15/11/2026, requires providers to mark synthetic outputs machine-readably and deployers to disclose deepfakes (image, audio or video that resembles real persons or things and would appear authentic), with lighter disclosure for evidently creative works; art. 113 still applies the Regulation generally from 2 August 2026 and art. 50 is not deferred (consolidated text of 27/07/2026, read 15/11/2026); high-risk rules moved to 2 Dec 2027 / 2 Aug 2028 and some art. 5 points apply from 2 Dec 2026. Check for amendments after 27/07/2026.
- [ ] Advertising law: an AI person implying a real customer or expert is a deceptive practice regardless of labels.
- [ ] Place the disclosure inside the piece where it is seen (on screen), not only in the caption, and in the audience's language.
- [ ] Record the owner's decision (what was disclosed, where, who decided, date) in the credits file; ads-manager toggle set to match.
- [ ] Brazil audience: check CONAR's code (https://www.conar.org.br/codigos?section=codigo; art. 28 ad identification, Anexo Q testimonials) and its influencer guide (PDF listed on the home page, not read).
