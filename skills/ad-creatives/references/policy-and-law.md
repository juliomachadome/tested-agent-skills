# Ad policy and EU law for creatives

Not legal advice; the buyer country's law and the project's legal notes prevail. An ad is commercial communication: everything that applies to the page applies to the creative. Detail on copy law: copywriting skill `references/eu-consumer-law.md`; on prices: offers skill `references/eu-consumer-law.md`.

## Deceptive practices (UCPD 2005/29 Annex I and national laws) [C][EU]
- Banned in any piece: false deadline or stock (no. 7); "free" for what requires paying something (no. 20); fake, commissioned or unverified reviews presented as customers' (no. 23b-c) [item numbers 23a-c not found in the 2005 base text read on 15/11/2026; added by a later amending directive, unverified, see BIBLIOGRAPHY 24]; posing as a consumer (no. 22); advertising disguised as content (no. 11); unproven outcome promises (art. 6).
- Platforms say the same: TikTok bans promising or exaggerating product effects, absolute terms and before/after comparisons (policy read 13/11/2026).

## Price in the creative
- **[C][EU] Any announced reduction needs the lowest price of the prior 30 days,** with the % computed on it (Directive 98/6 art. 6a; CJEU C-330/23). Strike-through, "−X%", "save" trigger it. In video the mandatory line must stay legible for reading time; if it does not fit, do not use a strike-through price.
- **[C] Price and conditions in the ad must match the page** (TikTok bans inconsistent promotion, price and discount info). Price changes? The creative goes offline the same day.

## Urgency
- **[C][EU] Only real deadlines, written out, without resetting timers or extensions.** A clock inside a recorded video is not a real countdown and looks like the fake kind: avoid.

## People, testimonials, creators
- **[C][EU] Testimonials only from real, verifiable customers with written image and voice release.** A hired actor or creator does not say "I use it" or "I bought it" unless true; paid content carries the platform's paid-partnership label and a disclosure in the video. National regulators fine both advertiser and hirer.
- **[C] Image rights are separate from copyright.** A stock licence covers the work, not the depicted person's consent to appear in advertising. Identifiable people in ads need a release; stock people only as background, not speaking, not "using the product", not looking like customers.

## AI-generated content
- **[C][EU] AI Act art. 50** (applicable from 02/08/2026): providers mark synthetic output in machine-readable form (art. 50(2); reports of a grace period to 02/12/2026 for systems already on the market; consolidated text of 27/07/2026 leaves art. 50 undeferred, while high-risk rules moved to 2027-28); whoever publishes a **deepfake** (generated or manipulated image, audio or video resembling existing people, objects, places or events that would pass as authentic) discloses it clearly (art. 50(4)). Commission code of practice and guidelines published mid-2026. [secondary legal summaries; verify the official texts]
- **[C] TikTok:** fully AI-generated images, video or **audio**, or real material significantly modified by AI, require the "AI-generated content" disclosure switched on in Ads Manager; the label shows under the video throughout. Synthetic-voice narration is fully generated audio. Ads without it may be rejected or restricted. (read 13/11/2026)
- **[M] Meta:** since 01/06/2026 Meta says it detects ad media created or edited with third-party AI and adds "AI info" under "About this ad" automatically; photorealistic people generated with Meta's tools get a label next to "Sponsored". Mandatory self-disclosure applies to political/social ads; blog claims that synthetic voice in commercial ads also needs self-disclosure since March 2026 were **not confirmed** on an official page. Check what Ads Manager asks before uploading. [search]
- **[C] Voice vendors have rules too.** Major AI-service codes of conduct require disclosing synthetic voices so nobody is led to believe a real person speaks, and forbid impersonation without consent and use for false advertising. Consequence: synthetic voice is "narration", never a named person; where to disclose is the owner's documented decision.
- **[M] Audiences dislike AI-looking ads and want labels:** in one representative national survey (n ≈ 1,050, Jan 2026), 32% said AI worsens ad reception, 15% improves it, 66% want AI ad content labelled. One country; the direction repeats elsewhere. [1-SRC]

## Stock libraries and third-party material
| Library | May | May not |
|---|---|---|
| Unsplash | commercial and non-commercial use without asking; credit optional | sell unmodified images; build a competing service |
| Pixabay | free use and adaptation; credit optional | sell unaltered; deceptive use; recognisable brands/logos in commercial use; recognisable people in illegal or immoral contexts; you must check whether third-party consent is needed |
| Pexels | free commercial use, no credit, editing allowed | sell unaltered clips; imply the depicted person endorses the product; identifiable people in offensive contexts |
(Licences read 13/11/2026; Pexels page returned 403, summary from an earlier read 07/11/2026.)

- Log author, link and ID of every clip in a credits file even when credit is optional. A stock phone showing another brand's app: blur or discard. Premium/"plus" tiers have their own licences.
- **[C] Ideas can be remade; files, soundtracks, faces and brands of others cannot.**

## Platform policies that take down course and info-product ads
- Promised or exaggerated results, absolute terms ("the best", "no. 1"), before/after (TikTok).
- Page inconsistent with the ad; mismatched price or discount (TikTok).
- Fake close or play buttons; elements imitating the interface (TikTok).
- Asserted or implied personal attributes (Meta).
