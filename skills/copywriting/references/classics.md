# Copy classics: principles in my own words and when to use them

No text is reproduced. Full citations are in `BIBLIOGRAPHY.md`. Tag: [C] widely corroborated, [M] old or single-source, practitioner evidence.

| Author and work | Principle | Use it when | Caution |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ogilvy, *Confessions of an Advertising Man* (1963), *Ogilvy on Advertising* (1983) | Research first; the headline does most of the work; promise a benefit, be specific, give facts; a campaign is judged by sales, not awards | Briefing a headline, long-copy pages, brand-consistent campaigns | Print-era numbers (readership ratios) are directional on the web [M] |
| Schwartz, *Breakthrough Advertising* (1966) | Copy channels existing desire; five awareness levels and five market-sophistication stages decide the opening and the claim | Always, in the 4-line diagnosis (SKILL.md section 1) | Stages are a model, not measured categories; judge them from market evidence |
| Hopkins, *Scientific Advertising* (1923) | Treat each ad as a test; measure with coupons or keyed responses; sell with specifics and reasons-why; avoid generalities and humor for its own sake | Setting up test variants, tracking per-version results | Method is timeless; examples are dated |
| Caples, *Tested Advertising Methods* (1932) | Headlines tested against each other differ by multiples; self-interest, news and curiosity tested best; test before scaling | Writing 5-10 headline variants and ranking by hypothesis | Same: direction from old tests, retest in your market |
| Sugarman, *The Adweek Copywriting Handbook* (2007) | Every element exists to get the next sentence read ("slippery slide"); build trust triggers, emotional reason then logical justification | Long sales pages, e-mail sequences, first-line writing | Some "psychological triggers" lists lack evidence [F]; keep only the ones you can make true |
| Halbert, *The Boron Letters* (written 1984; the edition catalogued here is 2013) and the Gary Halbert Letter | Know the starving crowd before the copy; clear offer to the right list beats clever writing; talk like one person to one person | Audience and list selection, direct-response letters, conversational e-mail | Anecdotal teaching, no data; dated tactics. Verify claims against modern rules |

How to combine:
1. **Diagnose** (Schwartz) then **research** (Ogilvy) then **write the headline** (Caples, Ogilvy) then **open the body** (Sugarman) then **ship as test** (Hopkins).
2. **Claims** still pass `references/eu-consumer-law.md`; classics predate consumer-law limits on claims, urgency and testimonials, and some of their tactics are now prohibited practices.
3. When a classic and current evidence disagree, current tested data wins.
