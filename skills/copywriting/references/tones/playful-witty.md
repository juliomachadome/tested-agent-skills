# Tone: playful and witty

## When it fits
Entertainment, games, media, youth and lifestyle brands, low-stakes consumer moments (loading screens, empty states, success messages, newsletters) where the reader came for fun and nothing is at risk.

## Word choices
- Wordplay and light surprise on top of a clear message; the joke is an extra, never the carrier.
- Familiar references only if they will not age within a season.
- Own the brand's running motifs; do not borrow another brand's.

## Sentence rhythm
Quick, with a setup and a turn. One joke per screen, not per sentence. Keep labels and buttons literal; put wit in headlines, empty states and success lines.

## Do
- Make the useful sentence first; add the joke after.
- Retire a joke when it repeats.
- Keep one tone per screen and drop the humour on errors, money, privacy and legal.
- Test the joke on a tired person reading on a phone.

## Avoid
- Humour at the reader's expense, sarcasm, in-jokes that exclude.
- Puns that depend on sound or spelling in a single language.
- Jokes about health, loss, money, identity, disasters.
- Wit that hides the price, the renewal or the cancel path.

## Before and after (same message: empty playlist)
Before: "You have no items in your playlist. Add items to begin."
After: "Nothing here yet. Your ears are waiting. Add a first track."

## Second example (same message: a successful save)
Before: "Your changes have been successfully saved to the system."
After: "Saved. Future you says thanks."

## Quick checks
- Does the plain meaning survive if the joke is removed?
- Is this a low-stakes moment (no money, health, privacy, failure)?
- Will the joke still read well in a screen reader and in translation?
- Have you used this joke or its structure on the previous screen?

## Risks
- Legal: humour never softens mandatory information; promotions and prizes keep their legal terms in plain text.
- Accessibility: wordplay, emoji and ASCII art can break screen readers and non-native readers; make sure the plain meaning survives alone. Respect reduced-motion and avoid flashing or animated text.
- Translation: puns and cultural references rarely survive; commission a local joke or use a neutral line.
- Failure mode: trying too hard. NN/g counts the attempt at humour, not its success: bad jokes still shape the tone.
