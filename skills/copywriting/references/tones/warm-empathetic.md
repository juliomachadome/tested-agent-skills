# Tone: warm and empathetic

## When it fits
Customer support, health and care contexts, education and coaching, apologies, cancellations, fail states, difficult news, and any reader who is worried, frustrated or vulnerable. Pairs with plain-trustworthy when money or health is involved.

## Word choices
- Acknowledge the situation in specific words: "Your payment failed and your lessons are paused" not "We understand your frustration".
- Plain, gentle, active: "We charged you twice. We fixed it."
- Offer choices: "If you'd like, we can...", and a clear human contact.
- Name the person's feeling only once and only when confident; do not diagnose.

## Sentence rhythm
Slower, short sentences, space between ideas. First line acts or acknowledges, second line explains, third gives the next step. No exclamation marks, no puns.

## Do
- Put the fix or the answer first, apology second, one specific apology.
- Give honest timing ("We'll reply within 1 working day").
- For health and education: encourage, never promise outcomes.
- Leave the exit easy and kind: "You can stop anytime in Settings."

## Avoid
- Template sympathy ("we value your feedback"), blame ("as stated in our terms"), minimising ("just").
- Emotional pressure in cancellations ("we'll be heartbroken").
- Humour in the first reply to a complaint.
- Guarantees of healing, success or safety.

## Before and after (same message: a failed renewal)
Before: "Payment declined. Your access has been suspended. Update your details immediately."
After: "We couldn't renew your plan today (the card was declined), so your lessons are paused. Your progress is saved. You can update the card in Settings, and if anything looks wrong, reply to this e-mail and a person will help."

## Second example (same message: reply to a complaint)
Before: "We apologise for any inconvenience this may have caused. Your satisfaction is our priority."
After: "You were charged twice for the same order, and that is our error. We have refunded the second charge (EUR 29), and your bank should show it in 3 to 5 working days. If it does not, reply here and I will follow up myself."

## Quick checks
- Does the first sentence either fix something or show we understood the exact problem?
- Is there a named next step with a time, and a person to reach?
- Would the reader feel respected if they read it while angry?
- Is anything promised that we cannot control (bank timing, outcomes)? State it as an expectation.

## Risks
- Legal: apology wording can matter in disputes; follow the project's complaints rules. Health claims and outcomes need evidence and regulated wording; vulnerable-consumer protections apply (the EU unfair-practices directive has a vulnerable-consumer provision, text not re-read here; Brazil CDC art. 37 section 2 bans advertising that exploits fear). Not legal advice.
- Accessibility: warmth is carried by clear structure, not decoration; keep text readable at a low reading level.
- Translation: formality and emotional register differ by culture; test with natives.
- Failure mode: syrup that delays the answer. Facts first.
