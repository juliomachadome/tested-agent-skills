# Tone: plain and trustworthy

## When it fits
Finance, insurance, health, legal, public services, anything where a wrong reading costs money or safety, and any reader who is anxious or in a hurry. Also the safe default for payment, cancellation and error screens in every brand.

## Word choices
- Everyday words over jargon; if a term is required (APR, excess, consent), define it once in the sentence.
- Concrete numbers, units and dates: "EUR 59 a year, charged on 3 March", not "great value".
- Active voice, "you" and "we" where it clarifies who does what.
- Say what is not included and what could go wrong, in the same place as the benefit.

## Sentence rhythm
Short and even: 12-18 words, one idea each. Lead with the point, then the detail. Lists for steps and options. No stacked clauses, no rhetorical questions. Calm, not cold.

## Do
- State the total price, the term and how to cancel before the final button.
- Give the next step and who to contact.
- Use official names for laws, products and fees.
- Write the way a good public-service content designer would: user need first (GOV.UK content design).

## Avoid
- Superlatives, urgency, exclamation marks, emoji, humour in money or health screens.
- Reassurance without a fact ("100% safe", "guaranteed results").
- Fine-print contradictions of the headline.

## Before and after (same message: annual renewal notice)
Before: "Great news! Your awesome plan is renewing soon, so don't miss out!"
After: "Your annual plan renews on 3 March for EUR 59 (VAT included). You do not need to do anything to keep it. To stop it, cancel in Settings before 3 March; you keep access until then."

## Second example (same message: a refund answer)
Before: "Per our policy, your request cannot be processed at this time."
After: "You can get a full refund until 20 March. Reply with your order number or use Settings, Billing, Request refund. We pay it back to the same card within 14 days."

## Quick checks
- Could someone read this once, on a phone, tired, and act correctly?
- Is every number followed by its unit, date and condition?
- Is the word for the same thing identical everywhere (plan, subscription, membership)?
- Does the sentence still work if read by a screen reader with no formatting?

## Risks
- Legal: claims about health or returns need evidence and regulator wording (EU unfair practices rules, `../eu-consumer-law.md`; Brazil CDC arts 36-38 (ads identifiable as ads, data kept to support the message, misleading ads banned, proof burden on the sponsor)). Not legal advice.
- Accessibility: this tone is the easiest to make accessible; keep headings and labels literal.
- Translation: travels well; keep terms consistent with local legal vocabulary rather than literal cognates.
- Failure mode: bureaucratic and cold. One human sentence ("We are sorry for the trouble") fixes it.
