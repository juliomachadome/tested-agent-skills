# Tone: luxury and minimal

## When it fits
Premium goods and services, design-led brands, high-priced professional services. The reader buys quality, time or status and reads little. Not for products where the reader needs to compare features or understand a complex offer.

## Word choices
- Few, precise nouns and materials: "Merino wool, Portugal", "Hand-finished, 14 hours".
- Understatement; let facts imply quality. No "luxurious", "exquisite", "premium" as adjectives.
- Process, origin and craft as proof; the price stated plainly.

## Sentence rhythm
Short. Often a fragment. Wide spacing, one idea per block, a calm period at the end of lines. No exclamation marks, no emoji, no urgency.

## Do
- Show the product, name the material, name the maker or the place.
- State price, delivery and returns clearly and early; opacity reads as cheap.
- Keep a human, courteous voice ("We will contact you within a day").
- Use "you" sparingly; address the reader with respect.

## Avoid
- Superlatives, "exclusive" without a real limit, invented heritage, fake scarcity ("only 3 left" when not true).
- Vague mystique in place of information the buyer needs.
- Small grey text for terms and fees.

## Before and after (same message: a leather bag)
Before: "Introducing our amazing, high-quality luxury bag! Buy now before it sells out!"
After: "The Tote. Full-grain leather from a family tannery in Tuscany. Hand-stitched in 11 hours. EUR 420. Free returns within 30 days."

## Second example (same message: shipping information)
Before: "Enjoy FREE premium express shipping on all exclusive orders!"
After: "Delivered in 2 to 4 working days in a sealed box. No shipping fee in the EU. Returns collected from your door within 30 days."

## Quick checks
- Is every adjective replaced by a material, place, time or number?
- Are price, delivery and returns visible without a click?
- Does the text pass contrast and size requirements at WCAG 2.2 AA?
- Is anything claimed as "limited" or "handmade" actually so, and documented?

## Risks
- Legal: "handmade", "made in", "limited edition" and material claims are regulated and must be accurate; a "limited" claim needs a real cap. Total price, taxes and returns stay visible despite the minimal design (`../eu-consumer-law.md`). Brazil CDC art. 37 on misleading information about origin and quality.
- Accessibility: pale grey on white and thin type fail contrast; minimal must still pass WCAG 2.2 AA; tiny buttons and image-only text are barriers.
- Translation: formality and politeness forms are a big part of the tone; use a native luxury copywriter.
- Failure mode: empty text that tells the buyer nothing. Minimal means fewer words, not fewer facts.
