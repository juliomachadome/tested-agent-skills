# Tone: friendly and casual

## When it fits
Consumer apps and services where the reader is a peer: habit, learning, food, travel, personal finance in low-stakes moments, onboarding and welcome messages. Works while the reader is calm.

## Word choices
- Spoken vocabulary, contractions, "you", first names only if you really have them.
- Small, specific warmth: "Nice, that took 40 seconds", not "Amazing!!!".
- Keep the product's own terms; do not invent cute names for settings.

## Sentence rhythm
Short sentences, some fragments, an occasional question. Vary length. Two lines per paragraph on mobile. Emoji at most one, never in errors, payments or legal text.

## Do
- Lead with what the reader gets or what happens next.
- Let the button label sound like the person talking ("Show me my plan").
- Mirror phrases from real users (voice-of-customer).
- Shift to plain and calm when something goes wrong.

## Avoid
- Forced slang, memes that age, fake intimacy ("hey bestie"), pressure through friendliness ("we'll miss you...").
- Jokes about money, health or failure.
- Exclamation marks in every line; they flatten emphasis.

## Before and after (same message: welcome e-mail)
Before: "Welcome to the platform. Your account has been successfully created. Please proceed to complete the onboarding."
After: "You're in. Pick the 3 topics you care about and we'll build your first week. Takes about 2 minutes."

## Second example (same message: a missed day in a streak app)
Before: "You have failed to complete your daily goal. Your streak has been reset."
After: "Missed yesterday, no problem. Your streak restarts today, and your 42 lessons are still there. Two minutes gets you back on track."

## Quick checks
- Would a friend say this out loud without cringing?
- Is the warmth specific (a detail about what they did) rather than generic?
- Does the tone drop to plain for money, errors and legal?
- Is there at most one emoji and one exclamation mark per screen?

## Risks
- Legal: friendliness cannot soften required wording; pay-button obligation text, price, renewal and withdrawal information stay explicit (`../eu-consumer-law.md`).
- Accessibility: emoji read aloud by screen readers; keep them rare and decorative. Idioms hurt non-native readers.
- Translation: casual register differs by country (informal address, slang); rewrite locally.
- Failure mode: sounds like every other app. Fix with one specific detail only this product has.
