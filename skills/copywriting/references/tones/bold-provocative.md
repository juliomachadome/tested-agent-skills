# Tone: bold and provocative

## When it fits
Challenger brands against an incumbent or a habit, with a product that really is different and proof to show. The audience must enjoy a stance. Not for regulated claims, grief or fear-based products.

## Word choices
- Strong verbs, short nouns, a clear enemy: the category's bad habit, not a group of people.
- Numbers as ammunition: "We cost 40% less than X's list price (their page, 12 Nov)".
- Claims concrete enough to be checked, because bold copy invites checking.

## Sentence rhythm
Short punches, fragments, contrast pairs ("Plans change. Fees shouldn't."). One bold line per screen, then plain support. Let white space carry the stance.

## Do
- Attack the status quo, the jargon, the complexity.
- Back every swing with proof within one scroll.
- Name competitors only with sourced, current facts (comparative advertising rules).
- Keep the next step plain: the button states the action.

## Avoid
- Insulting the reader or the reader's current choice.
- Fear, shame or fake enemies; absolutes you cannot prove ("the only", "never fails").
- Comparisons with stale prices or selected facts.

## Before and after (same message: a budgeting app)
Before: "Our app helps you manage your money better with smart features."
After: "Your bank shows you what you spent. We show you what to do about it. Free for the first 3 accounts."

## Second example (same message: a cancellation page)
Before: "We don't want to see you go! Are you sure? You will lose everything!"
After: "Cancel in two clicks. No call, no hoops, no guilt. Your data stays exportable for 30 days." (Provocation aims at the industry habit of hard cancellation, and the promise is checkable.)

## Quick checks
- Is the target a habit or category, not a person?
- Can every swing be verified by a stranger within a minute?
- Would you stand behind it printed next to a competitor's rebuttal?
- Does the next step stay plain and calm?

## Risks
- Legal: comparative advertising must compare like with like, verifiable features, no denigration; unproven superiority claims are misleading in the EU (Directive 2005/29/EC; the separate comparative-advertising directive was not re-checked) and breach CONAR art. 1 (honest and truthful). Not legal advice.
- Accessibility: fragments and all caps hurt reading; avoid all caps blocks.
- Translation: irreverence and cultural references rarely carry; test with locals.
- Reputation: provocation draws attention to the brand, including bad attention. Prepare for pushback.
