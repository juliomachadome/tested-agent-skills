# Style: value selling

## When to use
The buyer compares you with a cheaper alternative or the status quo and the argument is price. Works when the benefit can be measured in the buyer's own money, time or risk, and you can get their numbers.

## Core moves
1. Build a customer value proposition from three parts: the points of parity with the alternative, the points of difference that matter, and the resonating focus (the one or two differences the buyer values most). Avoid listing every benefit.
2. Get the buyer's baseline: current cost of the problem, volume, error rate, hours; mark each number as theirs, estimated, or assumed.
3. Compute value together: benefit minus the full cost of ownership (price plus switching, training, risk) over a stated period.
4. Show a range and the assumption that moves it most; let the buyer edit the inputs.
5. Offer scope options before any discount; trade, never give (`../playbooks.md` 6).

## Sample talk track (restated)
"Your figures were 40 returns a week at 25 minutes each. Our process cuts that by about half in similar teams, which you can test in the pilot. That is around 8 hours a week. At your loaded cost per hour it is worth more than the annual fee in about five months. Which of these inputs do you doubt most? Let us measure that one first."

## Avoid
- Invented or borrowed savings figures presented as the buyer's.
- Counting benefits twice or ignoring costs on the buyer's side.
- A value case the economic buyer has not seen.
