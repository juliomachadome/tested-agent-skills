# Style: Challenger (teach, tailor, take control)

## When to use
The buyer has a comfortable status quo and does not know what they are missing; you hold a real, provable insight; the account is large enough to justify research per buyer. Weaker in small deals and where trust is the main barrier; the research was on reps, not on every market.

## Core moves
1. Teach: open with an insight about the buyer's business that is new to them and leads to a problem your offer solves. It must be checkable (data, a comparable case).
2. Tailor: adapt the same insight to each role (finance sees cost, operations sees effort, IT sees risk).
3. Take control: discuss money and the decision process plainly; propose the path rather than waiting. Take control means clarity, not pressure.
4. Build consensus: the later work finds that many deals die from a stalled buying group, so give the champion material to teach colleagues, and use the "jolt" idea: reduce the risk of doing nothing, not only the risk of switching.

## Sample talk track (restated)
"Companies your size that renewed their cover on the usual date overpaid, on average, because quotes are cheapest eight weeks before renewal. [source, date] Your renewal is in October. If we start now you have two quotes in hand before the cheap window closes. Who else needs to see this, and what would they ask?"

## Avoid
- An insight you cannot source; a "provocation" that is only opinion.
- Contradicting the buyer to show strength; tension is built from evidence.
- Pressure, false deadlines, invented rival offers (never acceptable).

## Evidence and source
