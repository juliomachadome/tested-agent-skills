# Style: cultural adaptation (direct and indirect markets)

## When to use
Selling across countries or to buyers whose communication habits differ from yours. Use it to adjust delivery, never to switch ethics or to stereotype a person.

## Core moves
1. Treat culture as a hypothesis about tendencies in a group, not a rule for the individual in front of you; watch how this buyer actually speaks and mirror it.
2. Dimensions worth checking: direct versus indirect disagreement, low versus high context, how decisions are made (top-down or by consensus), attitude to hierarchy and time.
3. Examples. In a high-context, indirect setting a "that is difficult" may be a no; ask an open question ("What would need to be true for this to work?") and let the buyer save face. In a direct setting a plain "we are not buying" is a real answer and an open question may read as evasive; confirm in writing.
4. Decide-by-consensus buyers need more stakeholder material and a longer plan; hierarchical ones need the senior sponsor early.
5. Use a local colleague or interpreter for terms, titles and formality; translate meaning, not words.

## Sample talk track (restated; asking indirectly)
"We have a few options and I would like to learn which fits your team best. Is there any part of the plan that would be hard to explain internally? I would rather adjust it now than leave you with a proposal that does not travel well."

## Avoid
- Assigning traits to a person from their nationality.
- Gifts or hospitality beyond law and the buyer's compliance rules (anti-bribery laws apply anywhere).
- Assuming silence is agreement or objection; ask.
