# Style: consultative / SPIN

## When to use
Complex or considered B2B and high-value B2C purchases: deal size mid to large, cycle of weeks to months, a buyer who does not yet feel the full cost of the problem. Weak fit for small transactional sales, where the extra questions only add friction.

## Core moves
1. Agree an agenda and ask permission to ask questions.
2. Situation questions: few, researched in advance; never ask what a public page answers.
3. Problem questions: where does the current way hurt, and who feels it?
4. Implication questions: what does that cost in time, money, risk, other teams? This is where the buyer's urgency is built, by the buyer.
5. Need-payoff questions: "If that were fixed, what would change for you?" The buyer states the value.
6. Only then propose; match each proposed element to a need the buyer said aloud.
7. Close on a dated, advancing next step (a meeting with a named person), not a "let me think".

## Sample talk track (restated)
"Before I show anything, may I ask a few questions so I only cover what matters? ... You said invoices take nine days to reconcile. What does that delay cause downstream? ... Who else feels it? ... If that dropped to two days, what would it free up for your team? ... Then the part of our product worth your time is the matching feature; shall we book 30 minutes with your finance lead on Thursday to see it on your data?"

## Avoid
- Interrogating from a fixed list; follow what the buyer says.
- Implication questions that manufacture fear; they must uncover real cost.
- Presenting features before the buyer has named a need.
