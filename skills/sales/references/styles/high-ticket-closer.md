# Style: high-ticket closer calls (info-products, coaching)

## When to use
A priced offer of roughly mid to high ticket (programmes, coaching, mastermind) sold by a call to people who applied or booked. It is legitimate only with honest claims, a real fit check and a free decision.

## Core moves
1. Fit first: confirm the goal, the starting point, the budget range, and whether the offer can plausibly help; disqualify when it cannot.
2. Ask about the cost of staying where they are, in their words and numbers; do not exaggerate it.
3. Describe what the programme includes and what it does not, with results shown as typical, not guaranteed, and sourced.
4. State price, payment options and refund terms plainly and early; the buyer can take a day to decide.
5. If they hesitate, ask what is unclear; answer with facts or end kindly. Follow up once or twice, then stop.

## Sample talk track (restated)
"From what you told me, the goal is realistic but the programme will not do the work for you. Price is X, or three payments of Y; refund terms are in the contract, here is the page. You do not need to decide on this call; if it helps, I will send the summary and you can reply by Friday. What would you want to be sure of?"

## Avoid (hard limits)
- Pressure tactics: "last chance on this call", guilt, isolating the buyer from partner or adviser, ignoring a "no".
- False scarcity or fake deadlines and invented spots, rival offers or testimonials.
- Income or outcome claims you cannot document; hiding fees or auto-renewals.
- Pushing credit the buyer cannot afford.
- Withdrawal rights: distance and off-premises sales to consumers often carry a cooling-off period (EU: 14 days, with an exception for digital content or services once performance began with the consumer's prior express consent and acknowledgment). Other countries differ; check local law before writing terms.
