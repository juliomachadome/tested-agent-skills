# Methods in one page each

Written in my own words; read the books for depth. Evidence tags: C consensus, M serious but single or old, F practitioner.

## SPIN (Rackham, 1988, from about 35,000 sales calls) [M]
Situation (few, researched), Problem, Implication (what it causes), Need-payoff (buyer states the value). Best for complex, larger sales; the research did not cover tiny transactional sales. Used in `playbooks.md` section 1; question types, need types, advance vs continuation, objection prevention and the study's limits in section 7.

## Challenger (Dixon and Adamson, 2011; about 6,000 reps) [M]
Teach (an insight the buyer did not have that leads to your strength), Tailor, Take control. The 2015 follow-up stresses consensus-building within the buying group. Large B2B sample; fit varies by market and deal size; use the teaching, skip pressure.

## Gap Selling (Keenan, 2018) [F]
Problems sell, not products: current state, future state, gap, cost of the gap, cost of the fix. Discovery is diagnosis. Used in the gap record.

## MEDDICC [F]
Metrics, Economic buyer, Decision criteria, Decision process, Identify pain, Champion, Competition. A question list per deal; blanks are the work plan. No independent validation of the score known. Scorecard in `playbooks.md` section 2.

## Negotiation [C] / [M]
Fisher and Ury (Getting to Yes, 1981): interests not positions, options for mutual gain, objective criteria, BATNA [C]. Voss and Raz (2016): tactical empathy, labels, mirrors, calibrated questions [M], derived from hostage-negotiation practice; use as listening, not tricks.

## Prospecting and outbound [F]
Blount (Fanatical Prospecting, 2015): consistent daily prospecting blocks across channels. Ross and Tyler (Predictable Revenue, 2011): specialised outbound roles and metrics per step. Weinberg (New Sales. Simplified., 2012): a short problem-led approach to outreach and first meetings. All practitioner experience; none is controlled evidence.

## Early-market positioning [F]
Moore (Crossing the Chasm): win a narrow beachhead segment before expanding. Used for founder-led focus.

## Customer development (Blank and Dorf, The Startup Owner's Manual, 2012) [F]
A startup searches for a repeatable model before it executes one: test the problem, test the solution, then test-sell to early customers at close to full price before building a sales team. Practitioner method, no controlled evidence. Used in `founder-and-self-serve.md` section 7.

## Rapport and elicitation books [F]
Schafer and Karlins (The Like Switch, 2015, an ex-FBI behavioural analyst) on liking and rapport: ethical subset in `playbooks.md` section 8. Nolan (Confidential, 1999) on business intelligence and protecting your own information: ethical limits in section 9. Both are practitioner accounts; neither is a sales study.
