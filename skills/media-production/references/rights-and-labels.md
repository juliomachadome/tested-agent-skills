# Rights, likeness, music, voice, stock, live footage, AI labels

Not legal advice. Rules differ by country and platform and change: **check the rules of the country where you publish and the platform you publish on.** Brazil, the EU, the UK and the US appear below only as examples. Tags as in `SKILL.md`; dated facts read 18/11/2026.

## 1. Stock libraries: choose and use
| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Where to search | Photo and video libraries with a plain licence (free ones exist), paid libraries with indemnity for business use, music libraries (subscription or per track), sound-effect libraries, public-domain and Creative Commons collections |
| What to read | The licence page of the site and, for each file, the licence badge; screenshot the page and keep the download date, URL and author in a project log |
| Commercial vs editorial | Commercial use (ads, product, sponsored) needs commercial clearance and, for recognisable people, places or property, a release. Editorial-only files (news, commentary) cannot be used to sell or promote. If a file says editorial, do not put it in an ad |
| Attribution | Depends on the licence. A site may say credit is optional while Creative Commons BY requires it |
Dated facts: Pixabay's licence summary allows free use without attribution and modification, but not selling the content standalone without creative effort, not commercial use of content with recognisable trademarks or logos, not misleading or immoral uses especially with recognisable people, and it tells users to check third-party rights (bib. 6). Creative Commons describes six licences from most to least permissive; CC BY needs credit, CC BY-NC is non-commercial only (bib. 7). Freesound sounds are under CC0, CC BY or CC BY-NC, with a FAQ on claims after upload (bib. 8). One large free library (Pexels) blocked automated reading, so its terms were not read; check it directly.
Rules of thumb: recognisable face in an ad needs a signed release even in stock; logos, packaging, artwork and architecture in frame can carry trademark or design rights; CC BY-NC is never for ads; a free licence does not remove third-party rights; stock "content ID" claims can still happen on music, so keep licence proof.

## 2. Music
Four routes: licensed library (read plan terms for ads and paid promotion), commissioned or original (written agreement on ownership), platform commercial music library (business accounts are often restricted to it; paid promotion often needs separate rights), AI-generated music (read the plan: one voice vendor's pricing page lists commercial use of its music from its Starter plan, bib. 9; terms and indemnity not read). Trending sounds are for personal accounts on some platforms; check the business-account rule before using one. A cover or remix needs rights to the composition and the recording. Keep: track, author, licence, plan, date, URL. Before publishing ask: "can I use this music" = who owns the recording, who owns the composition, what use (organic, paid, TV, in-app), where (platform, country), how long, and does the plan cover it.

## 3. Likeness, releases and consent
- A real person's face, voice or distinctive likeness in an ad needs written consent that names the use, media, territory, duration and fee. Model release for each identifiable person; parent or guardian for minors; property release for private locations.
- Employees and customers: a casual "yes" is not consent; keep the signed form, and let people withdraw where law requires.
- Never generate or voice-clone a real person (celebrity, competitor, colleague, dead person) to endorse or say something they did not. It can breach publicity, defamation, consumer and platform rules, and the EU AI Act treats realistic manipulated content as a deepfake needing disclosure (section 6).
- Testimonials must be real, from real users, with consent; AI "customers" cannot give testimonials.
- Personal data in footage (faces, plates, screens) falls under the privacy law of the data subject's country (official regulator pages).

## 4. TTS and voice cloning
Stock or built-in synthetic voices: read the plan; commercial use may need a paid tier (one vendor's pricing page lists a commercial licence and instant voice cloning from its Starter plan at $6/month and professional voice cloning from its Creator plan at $22/month, 18/11/2026, with first-month promotions; bib. 9). Cloning: only your own voice, or a voice whose owner gave written, specific consent covering the use, term and payment; keep the consent file; never clone a voice from a clip you merely found. Disclose synthetic voice where the platform or law asks (section 6). Quality: record a clean 1-3 minute sample, read your script aloud first, fix names with phonetic spelling, listen for breath and pace, add a human check. Languages and accents vary; test the target market's dialect.

## 5. Live footage basics
Light: soft key near a window, no mixed colour temperatures. Sound first: a lav or close mic beats a better camera, record room tone 30 s. Frame at the delivery ratio, leave headroom for captions, shoot 3 s extra at each end, shoot inserts (hands, product, screen) for cutaways. Get releases on set. Back up the same day. Cut with `code-video-and-assembly.md`. Book/ideas: bib. 17, 20.

## 6. AI-content labels and disclosure
- **YouTube**: creators must disclose AI-generated or altered content that seems realistic, e.g. a real person doing or saying something they did not, altered footage of a real event or place, a realistic scene that did not occur, and (per the page) music that is the main focus of the video; set in the upload flow (bib. 3).
- **Meta (Instagram, Facebook)**: shows an "AI Info" label on content it detects as AI-made through industry-standard indicators or that the uploader says is AI-generated; Meta itself says the method is still evolving and can miss some AI-edited content, so disclose in the upload flow instead of waiting for detection (bib. 22).
- **TikTok**: its synthetic-media policy asks creators to label realistic AI-generated images, audio or video, using the in-app label or a caption or sticker, and it said it would test automatic labels for content it detects (2023 announcement, bib. 23). The current help-centre wording was not read; check it before publishing, and label when in doubt.
- **EU AI Act art. 50** (applies from 2 Aug 2026 per the text read): providers of systems that generate synthetic audio, image, video or text mark outputs in a machine-readable format; deployers of a system that generates or manipulates a deep fake (content resembling real persons, objects, places or events that would falsely appear authentic) disclose that it was artificially generated or manipulated; for evidently artistic, creative, satirical or fictional work the duty is limited to a disclosure that does not hamper the display (bib. 4). The secondary site read is not the Official Journal; check the current text and Commission guidance.
- **C2PA Content Credentials** is an open standard for recording origin and edits of media (bib. 11); some generators embed it. Do not strip it.
- Other examples to check: UK (consumer and advertising codes, CMA and ASA guidance), US (FTC rules on endorsements and deceptive ads; some state laws on deepfakes and voice), Brazil (CONAR, consumer code, LGPD, pending AI rules). Say "check the rules of the country and platform".
Practice: label in the platform field, add a short on-screen line for realistic content, add a profile line for AI series, keep a log of tools and plans per asset.

## 7. Ad-specific
Product claims must be true; AI footage must not show results the product does not give; sponsored content needs the platform tag and clear disclosure. Hooks, scripts and offer claims: `../../ad-creatives`, `../../viral-content`, `../../copywriting`.
