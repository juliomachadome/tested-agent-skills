# Diagnosis playbook: stories that do not work

Use after the minimum story in `SKILL.md` section 2. Each tree: symptom, likely cause, how to check, fix. Evidence tags as in `SKILL.md`.

## Step 0: what is the story for
Write five lines before diagnosing anything. If you cannot, ask.
1. **Goal**: inform, persuade, move, entertain, sell, retain a series audience.
2. **Audience**: who, what they already know, what they care about, why they would listen (not what you want to say).
3. **Medium**: talk, deck, page, video, podcast, series, interactive.
4. **Length**: seconds, minutes, episodes. Structure follows length (`variants/README.md`).
5. **End state**: what the audience should feel or do at the end (one verb).
Also check the raw material: is it a true account (facts to verify, people who need consent) or fiction? Is any element invented but presented as real (see ethics in `SKILL.md`)? If the evidence is invented, fix labelling before craft.
A cheap test for any draft: tell it aloud in 30 seconds to someone who does not know the subject, and ask them to state the want, the obstacle and the change. Wherever they stumble is the symptom below.

## 1. Nobody cares
- **Cause A**: no want. The character is described, not pursuing anything. **Check**: can you finish "She wants ___ by ___ so that ___"? **Fix**: choose a concrete, testable want; start the story the moment it is threatened.
- **Cause B**: no stakes. Nothing is lost if it fails. **Check**: ask "so what if she fails?" **Fix**: name the cost (money, trust, time, a person) and make it personal first; raise it once in the middle.
- **Cause C**: the audience has no way in (abstract, no person). **Fix**: one specific person, one moment, one detail the audience can picture.

## 2. Confusing
- **Cause**: too many characters or threads; the audience cannot tell who wants what. **Check**: count named characters in the first third and threads running at once. A short story carries one lead and one opposing force. **Fix**: merge characters, cut or defer subplots, repeat the want at each turn, use one setting per scene. If the length is fixed, cut threads rather than speed up.
- **Cause 2**: time jumps with no marker. **Fix**: anchor each scene with where and when in the first line.

## 3. Predictable
- **Cause**: no turn or reversal; the audience is ahead of the story. **Check**: mark every scene with the value at the start and end; if the value does not flip anywhere, nothing turns. **Fix**: insert a reversal (the plan works but costs something; the ally is the obstacle), or reveal information that reframes earlier scenes. Surprise must be inevitable in hindsight [C].
- Do not fix predictability with a twist that breaks the setup; check that the clue exists earlier.

## 4. Slow start
- **Cause**: setup before hook (background, credentials, scene-setting). **Check**: at what second or paragraph does the first question appear? In short video, if not in the first 3 seconds, treat it as late (see `skills/viral-content`). **Fix**: open at the moment of change or danger; deliver background in pieces when the audience needs it. Start close to the incident, leave early.

## 5. Flat ending
- **Cause A**: no payoff; the central question is never answered. **Fix**: answer it, even if the answer is bitter.
- **Cause B**: no change; the character ends as they began. **Fix**: show a different choice, a different belief, or a price paid, in an image rather than a statement.
- **Cause C**: the ending explains the moral. **Fix**: cut the last paragraph that explains; end on an action or image.

## 6. Preachy brand story (company as hero)
- **Cause**: the company is the lead; the customer is a prop; the story is founders' virtues. **Check**: who has the want, who decides? If it is the company, it is boasting [F]. **Fix**: the customer is the hero with a want and an obstacle; the company is the guide with empathy and a plan; the product is a tool at the turning point. A founder origin story can work when it shows the founder as a person with a problem they share with the customer, with a real cost. See `variants/brand-marketing.md`. No invented customer quotes; label composites.

## 7. Pitch that lists features
- **Cause**: features in product order; the audience is left to compute why it matters. **Check**: count sentences about the product before the first sentence about the audience's problem. **Fix**: problem, stakes, solution, proof, ask (`playbooks.md`). Each feature appears only as the answer to a stated pain. If the investor or buyer must infer the want, restructure.

## 8. Series loses viewers by episode
- **Check first**: with real retention or completion by episode, where is the drop? Episode 2 loss means weak first payoff; a slow decline means stakes are not rising; a cliff at one episode means a broken promise or a confusing recap. If you do not have per-episode data, say so and treat the causes as hypotheses.
- **Cause A**: stakes flat across episodes. **Fix**: each episode costs the lead something and raises the larger question.
- **Cause B**: no recap for new viewers. **Fix**: a five-second recap (who wants what, what changed).
- **Cause C**: episodes can be skipped. **Fix**: end each with a smaller payoff and an open larger question; plan the season end (`variants/series-interactive.md`).
- **Cause D**: promises not paid. **Fix**: track every open question in a bible and pay each before the season ends.

## 9. AI-generated story feels generic
- **Cause**: no specifics; default names, settings, adjectives, and a moral at the end. **Check**: replace the names and places with other ones; if nothing changes, it is generic. **Fix**: give the model a character sheet with a flaw, one concrete want, one unusual setting and three physical details; ask for scenes with a value flip, not a summary; forbid the closing moral; edit by hand for voice. Disclose AI use where required (`SKILL.md` ethics, `variants/short-form-ai.md`).

## Worked example: counting threads
A 90-second script has Ana (lead), her brother, a landlord, a new job and a lost cat. Five threads for 90 seconds is too many. Keep the lead, one opposing force (the landlord) and one want (keep the flat by Friday). Brother and job become a single line of stakes; the cat is cut. Check: can a stranger state the want, obstacle and stakes in 20 seconds? If yes, ship the draft.
