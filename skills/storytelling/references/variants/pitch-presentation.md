# Pitch and presentation stories

A pitch is a story where the audience decides how it ends. Evidence tags as in `SKILL.md`.

## 1. Shape
Problem as a person, stakes with a number, solution as the turn, proof, ask. Template in `../playbooks.md` 6. The audience must know the want (what are we trying to change) by the end of the first minute.

## 2. Slide titles as a story
Write the titles as full sentences that, read in order, tell the pitch ("Clinics lose 4 hours a week to scheduling", not "Problem"). Each slide carries one idea; the speaker carries the story. Practitioner guidance on presentations (Duarte on contrast between what is and what could be; Heath and Heath on concrete, emotional, simple, unexpected, credible) fits this: build tension between the present and the better future, then resolve it [F].

## 3. Open and close
Open on a specific moment (a person, a scene, a number that surprises), not on the agenda or your credentials. Close by returning to the opening image changed, then state the ask with a date.

## 4. Proof inside the story
Proof is the character getting the result: a customer, a pilot, a metric with its source. Never invent. If traction is thin, tell the learning story honestly: what you tried, what failed, what you will test next.

## 5. Handling objections
Raise the biggest objection yourself in the middle (the "all is lost" beat), then show how the plan survives it. A pitch with no visible risk reads as naive.

## 6. Check
Can someone who missed the talk state the want, the stakes and the ask from the titles alone? Run the revision checklist. Sales-process questions (discovery, negotiation) belong to sales; final slide or page wording that sells belongs to copywriting.

## 7. Worked example: from features to story
A feature-list opening: "Our platform has real-time dashboards, role-based access and an API." Rewrite for a clinic-operations buyer: "Last winter, a nurse manager at a 40-bed ward spent her Sunday rebuilding next week's rota by hand because two people called in sick. That is 6 hours, every sick call. We cut it to 20 minutes [if this is a measured pilot, name it and its source]. Here is how, and what we need to prove it in your ward." The features (dashboards, access, API) appear later as the plan.

## 8. Timing for short slots
- 3 minutes: problem and stakes 1 min, solution 1 min, proof and ask 1 min; no agenda slide.
- 10 minutes: add a second case and the main objection.
- 20+ minutes: add a mid-talk turn (a surprise number or a failure) so the middle does not sag.
Practise aloud with a timer; cut the slide with the lowest contribution to the want.
