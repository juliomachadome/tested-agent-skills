# Brand and marketing stories

A brand story is credible when the customer is the hero and the company is the guide [F]. Final copy goes to copywriting; this file shapes the story. Evidence tags as in `SKILL.md`.

## 1. Frame
Who is the customer, what do they want, what stops them, what does failure cost, what changes after? Then where the company enters: as the guide that shows empathy (we know this problem) and competence (here is the plan and the proof). The product appears at the turning point, not in the first paragraph.

## 2. Origin and founder stories
Founder origin stories work when they show a real problem the founder and the customer share, a real cost (time, money, failure) and a moment of change; they fail when they are a chain of virtues. A true setback told specifically earns more trust than a polished one [M, from craft practice; the book on storytellers cited in the bibliography gives many case studies, not controlled tests]. Use only events that happened; do not invent a garage, a failed customer or an epiphany.

## 3. Customer stories
Real customer, real permission in writing, claims checked, results typical or marked as not typical (consumer law on testimonials applies). Composite or illustrative customers must be labelled. Structure: before (want, obstacle), turning point (what they tried, what worked), after (specific result). No invented quotes.

## 4. Rewrite pattern for an "about us" page
1. Cut the opening that praises the company.
2. Open with the customer's moment.
3. Add the obstacle and what it costs.
4. Introduce the company as guide with one proof.
5. End with the customer's after state and one invitation.
Check: count "we" versus "you". If "we" dominates, rewrite.

## 5. Series and campaigns
A brand campaign can use a recurring character and engine (see `series-interactive.md`) but each piece stands alone. Keep claims provable; ad production specs belong to ad-creatives and distribution to viral-content.

## 6. Ethics
No fake urgency, fake scarcity, invented testimonials or AI-generated people presented as real customers. Disclose AI where required. Respect sensitive stories (health, loss): consent and no exploitation.

## 7. Worked example
Raw "about us": "Founded in 2015, we are a leading provider of smart scheduling. Our award-winning team delivers innovation." Problems: company is hero, no person, no want, no change. Rewrite: "Dana runs a three-chair dental practice. Every Friday she spends two hours calling patients who forgot Thursday. Each no-show costs a chair-hour she cannot recover. We built reminders after watching our first customer do exactly this; her no-shows fell from 11 to 4 a month in the first quarter [only if real, sourced and permitted]." If the figures are not real, say "illustrative" or leave the numbers out.

## 8. Checks
- Who has the want in the first paragraph? Is it the customer?
- Is every person, number and quote real and permitted, or labelled?
- Does the piece end on the customer's change, not a company boast?
- Is there one next step (not three competing calls)?
