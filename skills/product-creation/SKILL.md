# Product creation

Goal: the smallest product that delivers the promise, sold before it is polished, then grown one checkpoint at a time. Demand is market-research's job; here you build the cheapest version that gets a buyer the result and let sales, refunds and requests say what is next. Evidence tags: **[C]** consensus or law, **[M]** serious but single or correlational, **[F]** practitioner or vendor, **[X]** myth.

## 1. Start: project first, then five inputs
Read the current project's docs first (audience, past sales, brand rules, terms, stack, products); project rules win. Then fix, or ask for:
1. The validated pain and proof (who paid, pre-registered, replied). No proof: market-research.
2. The audience, its size, where the owner reaches it.
3. Real hours per week, money, skills, the deadline that matters (a wave, a date).
4. The promise in one sentence: who gets which result in how long.
5. What is already built or sold.

## 2. CORE 1 - Build-mode selector
Start fast; step up only when data asks **[F]**.

| Mode | Time | What it is | Use when |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fast | hours to 2 days | Presale, simple PDF or Notion or folder, concierge (by hand for the first buyers) | Signal not proven by money yet, short window, price low, risk low |
| Standard | 1-2 weeks | Finished core product, one clear path, basic onboarding, real checkout | First buyers paid and asked for more, window allows, price mid |
| Polished | weeks or more | Full production, brand, automation, support system, bonuses | Repeat demand, refunds under control, margin proven, higher price |

Choose by five inputs, then say which mode and why:
- **Signal strength**: paid presales or waitlist deposits beat likes. Likes only: fast.
- **Window**: viral waves decay in days **[F]**; if the window is under two weeks, fast is the only mode that catches it.
- **Price**: higher price, higher expected finish.
- **Risk**: refunds, reputation, a public promise. High risk lifts the mode one step.
- **Effort versus return**: build hours against expected sales in the window (`references/playbooks.md` 1; numbers belong to business-model-finance).

**Fixed limits in every mode** **[C]**: deliver exactly what was promised and by the date promised; the consumer withdrawal right stays and depends on the buyer's country (see below; refunds and law go to customer-support); no fake deadlines, scarcity or counters (offers); do not promise results you cannot show. Fast cuts polish, never honesty.
**Country rule**: check the consumer, tax and product-safety law of the country where the buyer is and where you sell from; general information, not legal advice. Examples **[C]**: Brazil CDC art. 49, 7 days for distance contracts; EU Directive 2011/83/EU art. 9, 14 days, except digital content once performance began with prior express consent and acknowledgement of losing the right (art. 16(m), matters for PDFs); UK Consumer Contracts Regulations 2013, 14 days, similar exception (reg. 37). US: the FTC page read shows no general federal right to return online purchases; sellers state terms. Detail: `references/launch-and-quality.md` 5.

## 3. CORE 2 - Phases and checkpoints
One product first. Do not start the second until the first has paid for itself or a clear stop rule fired.
- **Phase 0 Sell**: presale or concierge; first 10-30 buyers; deliver by hand.
- **Phase 1 Deliver**: fulfil, watch the first value moment (section 6), collect questions.
- **Phase 2 Checkpoint** (fixed date, `references/playbooks.md` 3): sales vs plan, refund rate, repeated requests, margin after fees and support time, load per 100 buyers. Decide: improve, step up the mode, add the next rung, or stop.
- **Phase 3 Ladder**: add a rung only when the previous one pays for itself and buyers ask (`references/playbooks.md` 4). Typical order: free entry, kit or ebook, course or cohort, community or mentoring, done-for-you, software **[F]**.
Rules of thumb (**[F]**): refunds above about 10% on a digital product mean a promise-delivery mismatch; the same request from 3+ buyers is a feature; over about one support hour per ten buyers, the product or onboarding leaks.

## 4. Product types (see `references/product-types.md`)
- **Simple digital** (PDF, ebook, template, folder, Notion): hours to days, near-zero cost, margin about 90% before fees, instant delivery; risks: copying, low perceived value.
- **Content and service** (course, cohort, community, mentoring, done-for-you): days to weeks; cohorts presell; your time caps income; refunds and completion are the risks.
- **Software** (no-code tool, app, SaaS): weeks to months; highest ceiling and cost; validate by concierge or no-code first (PRD: product-management; app stores: mobile-app-engineering).
- **Physical** (print on demand, dropshipping, own stock): tied-up cash or thin margins, shipping, invoices, returns; regulated categories need certification or registration before sale (INMETRO, ANVISA in Brazil; CE, UKCA marking; FDA, CPSC in the US): check the country's official pages and a local professional.

## 5. MVP scope cutting
List what the buyer needs for the promised result, keep only what the first result requires **[M]**. Cut order: bonuses, polish, extra formats, community, app. Keep: the core result, a first step, a way to ask for help. Concierge test: deliver by hand to five people before building. Ask buyers: "what almost stopped you from finishing?".

## 6. Delivery, onboarding and support
- **Platforms**: Brazil: Kiwify, Hotmart (checkout, delivery, affiliates, Pix, boleto). Global: Gumroad (simple), merchant-of-record services such as Paddle (collect and remit VAT or sales tax; verify availability), Stripe (own checkout, you carry tax and invoices), Shopify for physical, own site (control, more work). Choose by the buyers' country, payment methods and who collects tax; compare live fees (`references/product-types.md`).
- **Onboarding and first value**: define the first value moment (first small result) and make it happen within 24 hours: a welcome message with one step, not a library tour. Software onboarding: ux-design (or an onboarding skill if installed).
- **Support load**: before launch set one inbox, three canned answers, a "start here" page, a promise you can keep. Hours = buyers x tickets x minutes; replies and refunds: customer-support.
- **Versioning**: name versions (v1.0), keep a changelog, fix errors free, state which updates are included, keep old buyers' access.

## 7. When to kill
Stop (or freeze and sell as is) after one full checkpoint cycle when sales are below the floor set before launch, refunds stay high after two fixes, support cost exceeds margin, or you no longer want this audience. Rules: `references/playbooks.md` 5.

## AI tells to avoid (check while answering, not after)
- Generic advice that would fit any business, instead of the user's own numbers.
- Benchmarks or market sizes stated as fact, with no source.
- Round-number assumptions with no source or range.
- Frameworks named (SWOT, Canvas, Van Westendorp) but not applied to the case.
- "It depends" without saying on what and what to do.
- Long option lists with no recommendation.
- Confident tone on unverified fees, laws or rates.
Fix: use the user's data, show the arithmetic, give a range and the assumption, then one recommendation with its reason and the next check. Say what you chose and why in one line; a choice without a reason is removed.
