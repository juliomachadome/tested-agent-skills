# Diagnosis playbook: product creation

Step 0 comes first. Evidence tags as in SKILL.md.

## Step 0: is the data trustworthy?
- How many buyers, over how many days? Under 30 buyers, treat rates as direction, not fact (3 refunds of 20 is 15%, one more changes it by 5 points).
- Are refunds counted by order date or by refund date? Use cohorts by order date; a launch week looks clean until the 7-day window closes.
- Was the buyer mix one wave (viral traffic, cold) or the warm list? Cold traffic refunds more; split before concluding.
- Did the promise change between ad, page and product? Compare the three texts side by side.
- Is "completion" measured (platform data) or guessed from comments?

## Tree 1: built too much before selling
Symptom: weeks of work, few or no sales. Check: was there any paid signal before building (presale, deposit, replies)? Cause A: no signal, only likes -> stop building, run the 48-hour presale (`playbooks.md` 2) on what exists. Cause B: signal existed but the build outgrew the window -> ship the cut version now (list what the first result needs, drop the rest, `SKILL.md` 5). Cause C: perfection habit -> set a ship date and a "good enough" checklist of five lines. Fix order: sell what exists, then build only what buyers ask for.

## Tree 2: sold, but deliveries are late
Check: how many open orders, hours per delivery, hours per week available. Example: 40 buyers x 2 h concierge = 80 h; with 10 h a week that is 8 weeks, not the 7 days promised. Cause A: promise exceeds capacity -> message buyers with a new honest date and an option to refund (**[C]** in most countries: late delivery of a promised item is a consumer-law issue; route to customer-support). Cause B: manual steps -> batch, template or automate the repeat step. Cause C: sold more than the cap -> close sales at a number you can serve; do not keep selling what you cannot deliver.

## Tree 3: refunds high (e.g. 18%)
Check in order: (1) when do refunds arrive (day 1-2 is a mismatch of promise; day 6-7 is no use or no result); (2) reason codes or the replies; (3) by traffic source; (4) completion of refunders vs keepers.
- Promise mismatch: page says more than the product does -> fix the promise with copywriting before touching the product.
- Wrong buyer: cold viral traffic, impulse buy -> add a clearer "who this is not for" and a first-lesson preview.
- No first value in 24 hours -> onboarding fix (`SKILL.md` 6).
- Product too thin or too hard -> add the missing piece buyers name most (repeated requests), not what you imagine.
Never fix refunds by hiding the withdrawal right or making refunds hard **[C]**: where consumer law gives that right (EU and UK 14 days, Brazil 7 days for distance sales; see `launch-and-quality.md` 5) it is unlawful and it raises chargebacks. Target: re-measure on the next 50 buyers.
Worked numbers: 260 buyers, 47 refunds = 18.1%; cold ad buyers 41 of 190 = 21.6% versus newsletter buyers 6 of 70 = 8.6%; 29 of 47 refunds on day 1-2 point to the promise, not the product.

## Tree 4: buyers do not finish or use it
Check: where do they stop (lesson, page, step)? Median days to the first action? Cause A: first value comes too late -> move a quick win to the first 10 minutes. Cause B: too long -> cut modules; a 3-hour result beats a 12-hour library. Cause C: no nudge -> a three-message sequence (day 1 start, day 3 stuck?, day 7 finish line); no fake urgency. Cause D: wrong format for the buyer (video vs checklist) -> ask five non-finishers one question. Completion of an unfinished self-paced course is often below half **[F]**; compare to your own baseline, not to a headline number.

## Tree 5: support overwhelming
Count tickets by topic for two weeks. The top three topics usually cover most volume. Cause A: unclear delivery or access (login, download) -> fix the access email and "start here". Cause B: missing answer in the product -> add it. Cause C: low price, high-touch buyers -> raise the price (offers) or move help to a group or a paid tier. Cause D: you answer everything personally -> canned answers, a response window, an FAQ. Example: 400 buyers x 0.3 tickets x 6 min = 12 h; at 19 EUR and 400 units the revenue is 7,600 EUR before fees, so 12 h is fine; at 1.2 tickets it is 48 h and the product is leaking.

## Tree 6: product ladder with no takers
Check: take rate of the next rung among buyers who finished the first (not among all buyers); asked about the need directly. Cause A: the first rung did not deliver a result, so there is no trust -> fix it before adding rungs. Cause B: next rung solves a different problem -> rebuild it as the obvious next step of the same journey. Cause C: price jump too large (R$47 to R$1,997) -> add an in-between rung or a sample. Cause D: offered at the wrong moment -> after the first result, not at checkout. Cause E: only 40 finishers exist, so 2% is under one buyer -> too small to judge; wait or ask directly. Stop adding rungs until one pays for itself.

## Tree 7: stuck in polish
Symptoms: the date moved twice, no sale since the last version. Check: what would a buyer lose if you shipped today? If nothing important, ship. Cause: fear of reputation -> a "version 1" label, a free fix promise, and a small first audience. Set the ship date, freeze scope, put polish ideas on a v1.1 list. Calculate hours spent vs expected extra sales; polish rarely beats a first sale.

## Tree 8: sales only from the launch wave (fit unclear)
Check: orders per day by source for 30 days, share of finishers, repeat or referral orders, unprompted requests. Cause A: sales were the wave and no one finishes -> the promise attracted impulse buyers; fix onboarding and promise before more traffic. Cause B: finishers exist but none refer or return -> run the survey and interviews in `launch-and-quality.md` 2 with finishers only. Cause C: all signs weak after two changes -> `playbooks.md` 5 (stop). Do not read a 40% survey result from fewer than 40 answers as fact.

## Tree 9: leaks, copies and licence trouble
Check: is it your exact file, who had access, how many copies, what was the loss in sales (not the number of downloads). Act in the order of `launch-and-quality.md` 8; for a licence claim against you (font, image, template) stop distributing the file, check the licence you held, fix or replace the element and get a professional if money or a demand letter is involved (`launch-and-quality.md` 4).
