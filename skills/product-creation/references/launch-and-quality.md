# Launch and quality: product creation

Evidence tags as in SKILL.md. Legal points are general information, not legal advice: the rule is to check the consumer, tax and product-safety law of the country where the buyer is (and where you sell from), and ask a professional there.

## 1. Pre-launch quality checklist (digital product)
Run it the day before the first delivery. Any "no" on 1-4 blocks the launch.
1. The promise sentence matches the ad, the page and the product (read the three side by side).
2. Someone who is not you got the first result using only the delivery email and the start page (test with one real person, 30 minutes).
3. Every link, file, login and download works on phone and laptop, in a fresh browser.
4. Refund terms, delivery date, what is included and what updates are included are written on the page.
5. Facts, numbers and examples checked; sources named where you rely on them.
6. Files carry a version (v1.0), a date and your contact; metadata of files stripped of private data.
7. Licences and credits for everything you did not create are on file (section 4).
8. Accessible basics (section 6).
9. Support set up: one inbox, three canned answers, a "start here" page.
10. Tax, invoice and payout set up with the platform (section 7).
Quality bar for fast mode: correct and usable, not pretty. Typos and spacing can ship as v1.0.1; a wrong claim or a broken first step cannot.

## 2. Product-market fit signals after launch
Do not call it fit from sales alone. Read these together on the same buyer cohort, at the phase review:
- **Repeat or expand behaviour**: buyers come back, buy the next product, refer others unprompted. Referral share of orders is cheap to measure with a one-line "how did you hear" field.
- **Retention or use**: for recurring products, month-3 retention of a cohort; for one-off, the share that reaches the first value moment and finishes.
- **Refund and complaint rate** under your ceiling after two fixes.
- **Sean Ellis survey** **[F]**: ask recent users "how would you feel if you could no longer use this?" with very disappointed, somewhat, not disappointed; practitioners read about 40% "very disappointed" as a fit signal. It is a heuristic from one practitioner's start-up sample, not a law; with fewer than about 40 answers it is direction only. Ask users who reached the first value, not everyone.
- **Unprompted requests** from three or more buyers for the same thing.
Fit is weak when sales come only from the launch wave, finishers are few, and nobody asks for more. Then work on promise, audience and onboarding before adding features. Experiments and funnels go to growth-analytics; software discovery to product-management.

## 3. Naming the product
Name for the result or the user, not for cleverness: a name that says who it is for and what it does survives a cold audience. Checks before printing it on anything: search the name and the domain or handle; search the trademark register of the countries you sell in (a professional can do the clearance); say it aloud to three people; avoid terms that imply a health, financial or guaranteed result you cannot prove. Offer and mechanism naming belongs to offers; page words to copywriting.

## 4. Content IP, licences and AI-generated material
Rules of thumb **[F]**, then check the licence text itself and the law of the country (professional for anything commercial and large):
- You may only sell what you made or hold a licence to resell inside a product. Many templates, fonts, icons, stock photos, music and plugins forbid resale of the file itself, or allow use inside a finished work but not redistribution as a template. Read the licence type (personal, commercial, extended) and keep a credits file with licence and purchase proof.
- A template built from someone else's template, a screenshot of another product, or a copied course outline is a risk even if edited.
- Open licences (for example Creative Commons variants) differ: share-alike and attribution terms can bind your whole product.
- AI-generated text, images or code: the tool's terms say what you may do commercially; whether the output is protected by copyright varies by country and is unsettled in several, so do not promise exclusivity of AI-made material; add your own selection and editing; do not generate imitations of a named living creator's style or a brand; disclose AI use where a platform, law or buyer expectation requires it.
- Your own work: put a copyright line, the licence you grant (section 5) and the year on the product.
- Claims: testimonials and results shown must be real and be allowed to be used (offers, copywriting).
Trademark and image rights of people (photos of clients) need consent; personal data in examples goes through the privacy law of the data subject's country (official regulator pages).

## 5. Terms of use and licence for a digital product
Short written terms on the page and in the delivery email, in plain language: what the buyer gets and may do (personal use, one team, a client project), what is forbidden (resale, sharing the file or account, republishing), what updates are included and for how long, how support works, refunds and the withdrawal right as the buyer's country gives it, the seller's identity and contact, and the governing law. Consumer rights cannot be waived by your terms in most countries, so write terms that sit on top of them, never against them. Get a lawyer's template reviewed once; reuse it.
Withdrawal for digital content, worked examples **[C]** where read (bibliography): EU 14 days, with an exception for content not on a tangible medium once performance has begun with the consumer's prior express consent and acknowledgement of losing the right (Directive 2011/83/EU art. 16(m)); the UK has the same shape (regulation 37 of the Consumer Contracts Regulations 2013); Brazil gives 7 days for distance contracts (CDC art. 49); in the US, no general federal right to return an online purchase was found in the FTC page read, sellers must state their return terms, and state law and marketplace policies apply. If you rely on the exception, the checkout needs a separate unticked box with timestamped record and a confirmation on a durable medium (email), not one line "you agree to the terms". Refund cases go to customer-support.

## 6. Accessibility of digital deliverables
Aim at the WCAG 2.2 principles (perceivable, operable, understandable, robust), level AA, for web delivery; for PDF and documents the same ideas apply **[F]**: real headings and a reading order, text that is text (not images of text), alt text on meaningful images, contrast, a readable font size, captions or a transcript for video and audio, no colour-only meaning, links with descriptive text, a tagged PDF where your tool allows. Some jurisdictions require accessibility for certain products and sellers; check where you sell. Cheap test: read the file with a screen reader or text-to-speech for two minutes and navigate by keyboard only. UI detail goes to ui-design and ux-design.

## 7. Tax, invoice, platform and payout
Tax on digital sales (VAT, GST, sales tax, local rules and registration thresholds) depends on the buyer's country and yours. Two ways: use a **merchant of record** platform that sells to the buyer, collects and remits the tax and handles refunds and chargebacks (fees are higher; check availability for your country and that the vendor is still operating), or use a payment processor or local checkout where you carry the tax and invoice duties. Choose by the buyers' country, the payment methods they use (cards, wallets, bank transfer, local instant payment) and who collects tax. Invoice and fiscal-document duties for digital sales (for example in Brazil an invoice per local rules) go to business-model-finance and an accountant.

## 8. Piracy and leak handling
Expect some. Proportionate steps **[F]**: deliver through an access-controlled area or personalised links rather than an open file; visible watermark with the buyer's email on paid files (discourages casual sharing, not a determined copier); do not use heavy DRM that punishes honest buyers; keep a price and a free sample that make buying easier than hunting; monitor by searching distinctive phrases. When a leak appears: confirm it is your file; contact the host with a takedown notice under the law of the host's country (professional if unsure); trace the source if watermarked and warn or cut the account per your terms; do not accuse the buyer in public; do not collect more personal data than needed. Decide the cost: hours on takedowns beat sales lost only for high-value products.

## 9. Physical products, in brief
Regulated categories need certification, registration or marking before sale: for example INMETRO or ANVISA in Brazil, CE or UKCA marking in Europe and the UK, FDA or CPSC rules in the US. Also shipping, returns, labelling, import duties, product liability and insurance. Check the official pages of the country where you sell and ask a professional there before you list a product. Brazil example read: the INMETRO table of compulsory programmes lists about 150 product groups (e.g. plug adapters, baby walkers, cribs, school supplies, party items) with the mechanism and ordinance for each, and points some rows to Anvisa or other regulators (source 16; table last updated 2022, confirm the current ordinance).
