# Account structure for small budgets

- **[C] One campaign per objective, the fewest ad sets, broad audience, several different ads.** Meta, TikTok and Google docs say the same in different words: each ad set needs events to learn.
- **[MATH] How many ad sets fit:** weekly budget ÷ (50 × CPA). Below 1 means one ad set, and "learning limited" is expected.

| Daily budget ÷ CPA | What fits |
|---|---|
| up to 3 | 1 campaign, 1 ad set, 3-5 ads; 2 ad sets only to compare two entry points |
| 3-7 | 1 campaign, 1-2 ad sets, weekly batch of 3-4 new ads |
| 7-15 | exits learning; scaling campaign + creative test on a slice up to 20% |
| above 15 | second platform, separate remarketing, page tests with real sample |

- **Budget at ad-set level (ABO) vs campaign level (CBO):** ABO when the question is to **compare** two things at equal spend (two entry points, pages, countries); CBO when the question is to **sell** and the total is what you read. Mixing both questions in one campaign gives the wrong reading.
- **Optimisation event: purchase from day one.** In low ticket, purchases are frequent enough to learn; optimising for clicks or visits brings people who click and do not buy. [F] Meta suggests a more frequent event when an ad set is limited [search], and one incrementality study found mid-funnel events only ~5% less efficient for high-volume advertisers [M][1-SRC]. Rule: purchase; if an ad set has fewer than ~10 purchases a week for two weeks, test "lead" or "initiate checkout", judging only by backend purchases, never cost per lead.
- **Funnels with a free step (quiz, test, sample):** a cheap lead deceives; whoever leaves an e-mail is not whoever pays. Optimise for purchase, use the lead as a diagnostic. Switch only if the purchase happens days later by e-mail and the platform cannot see it.
- **Audience: broad with few controls.** Country, minimum age if the product needs it, language only where many residents do not read the ad's language. Interests and lookalikes as suggestions or not at all. In a new account, the creative does the targeting.
- **Automatic placements.** Judge at ad-set level, not per placement. Exception: a format that does not exist for a placement (no vertical version for Reels).
- **Bids: highest volume at the start.** Cost caps and bid caps need history to set and do not work with Meta's creative testing tool. Practitioners validating with bid caps equal to ticket and to ideal CPA across two accounts [F][1-SRC] use a technique for operators with volume and many accounts.
- **[X for small accounts] "Have at least two ad accounts."** Comes from operations running dozens of offers. For a single verified seller, one well-kept account is worth more. Opening accounts to circumvent bans or rejections violates circumvention policies and usually takes everything down.
- **Separate remarketing only with scale** (daily budget above ~15× CPA) or when a message only fits people who saw the page (real deadline, objection answer). Under consent-gated pixels the list is smaller than real traffic.
- **Names are tracking keys.** Campaign and ad names follow the project's UTM convention so spend matches sales in the backend; one link per ad.
- **Account hygiene:** business verification, two-factor auth, a second trusted admin (a blocked account with a single admin is the costliest risk), verified domain, payment method with a limit, account spend limit equal to the month's budget. Currency and time zone cannot change later: use the selling currency and the audience's time zone so dashboard days match backend days.
- **Rejections:** read the reason, fix the piece, request review once. Never re-upload unchanged; never open a new account to get around it.
- **[EU] Beneficiary and payer are public.** Decide what goes in those fields before the first ad; for a natural person selling under their own name, ask a lawyer. Realistic AI content (face, voice imitating someone, scene that looks filmed) needs platform labels and, in the EU, AI Act disclosure (see ad-creatives).
