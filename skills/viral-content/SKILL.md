# Viral content

**Goal is business, not views.** Every piece is judged by what it moves next: follows from the right audience, profile visits, link clicks or DMs, leads, sales. A viral post that brings the wrong audience or no next step is a cost. Before writing, name the conversion the piece serves and the one metric that proves it (`references/virality-to-conversion.md`); image and sound levers: `references/visual-audio.md`.

Reach is a side effect of a viewer's small decisions: stop, stay, finish, react, pass it on. A feed ranks what viewers do; you design what makes them do it. No one can promise a hit: social influence makes outcomes unequal and hard to predict (bib. 11, 12), so plan for volume, honest tests and the median. Tags: **[C]** consensus or law, **[M]** serious but single, lab or correlational, **[F]** platform or practitioner statement, **[X]** myth. Sources not read in full: `references/BIBLIOGRAPHY.md`; test on your account.

## 1. Start: project, then evidence
Read the current project's docs first (audience, offer, brand voice, claims rules, past post stats); project rules win. Then ask for the minimum: platform, account age and followers, niche, the metric that worries them, stats of at least 10 recent posts (views, average watch time or retention at 3 s, shares, saves, follows, post time, length), and the goal (reach, follows, leads, sales). State assumptions where data is missing. One post proves nothing; compare medians over 10+ posts.

## 2. The mechanism ladder (the core)
| Decision | What the viewer is asking | Mechanism | Evidence | Design lever |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Stop | Is something here unexpected or about me? | Expectation violation, salient motion, faces | [M] bib. 19, 20 | A frame that breaks a pattern or shows a person, a moving thing, one clear idea in 1-2 s |
| Stay | What happens, and how? | Curiosity: a felt gap between known and wanted (Loewenstein) | [M-C] 16, 17 | State the question or stakes early, withhold the answer, never fake it |
| Finish | Am I inside this? | Narrative transportation | [M] 18 | Story, second person, concrete scenes, one thread |
| Remember, rewatch | How did it end? | Peak-end, fluency, repetition | [M] 23, 24, 25 | Strong last beat, consistent look so it is easy to process |
| Share | What does it do for me and my friends? | Arousal, social currency, practical value, identity | [M] 4, 5, 7, 10 (later test of arousal: 6, result unread) | Make the sender look smart, funny or caring, or save a friend time |
| Save | Will I need this later? | Utility | [M] 4, 10 | Steps, lists, checklists |
| Follow | Do I want more of this person? | Series promise, repeated exposure, parasocial bond | [M] 25, 26 | A stated next part, one voice, a reason in the first seconds |
| Buy | Do I trust and need this? | Trust, awareness level | [F] | `references/virality-to-conversion.md` |
The Zeigarnik effect is weaker than the folk version (mixed replication, 21, 22): use curiosity, not "the brain must finish it" **[X]**. High-arousal emotion (awe, anger, anxiety, amusement) is associated with sharing more than low-arousal (sadness, contentment) **[M]**, observational for news (4). A format copied without its mechanism does not transfer: see `references/field-study-10-reels.md`, where 10 videos above 1M views are read mechanism by mechanism (one niche, winners only: hypotheses, not a recipe).

## 3. What the platforms say [F]
Instagram predicts, for Reels, the chance of a reshare, watching to the end, a like and a visit to the audio page (29). TikTok weights strong interest signals (finishing a longer video) above weak ones, and may not recommend spam, new or under-review uploads (30). YouTube recommends by each viewer's habits and what they value (31); Shorts views count every start or replay since 31 Mar 2025 and "engaged views" is separate (32). No weights are published; treat other "the algorithm rewards X" claims as **[X]** until tested. Posting frequency: no source gives a number; test it (`playbooks.md` 5).

## 4. Diagnose (existing posts)
Run `references/diagnosis-playbook.md`: Step 0 (metric, platform, account age, niche, sample size, posting time), metric formulas, then the tree: low views; drop in the first 3 s; drop mid-video; views but no follows; views but no sales; low shares; one hit then nothing; sudden reach drop; negative comments; works on TikTok but not Reels. Output: data quality, the leak, most likely and second cause, one change per next post, what you could not verify. Under about 10 posts: too noisy to conclude.

## 5. Create (new posts)
1. One viewer, one situation, one promise. 2. Choose the mechanism per beat before the format: what makes them stop, what keeps them, what makes them send it. 3. Pick a hook from `references/playbooks.md` section 1 that fits that mechanism and is true to the video. 4. Script (templates in section 2), edit with the checklist (section 3). 5. Pass the checks: does the first 3 s deliver what the caption promises; would one person send it to one named friend, and why; what is the next step for someone who liked it. Use real material; label AI content as platform and law ask (section 8).

## AI tells to avoid (check while creating, not after)
- Every hook from one formula ("POV:", "Nobody talks about", "Stop scrolling"); vary hooks by mechanism.
- Stock trend phrasing with no reason tied to this video.
- Captions that restate the video; add what the footage cannot show.
- Hashtag walls; a few tags that name the topic.
- AI voice or visuals with no reason and no label.
- "Story time" with no real detail (name, place, number, what was said).
- Every script ending "follow for more"; end on the payoff or a named next part.
- Fix: real people, real detail, the creator's own voice, a reason for each choice. Say what you chose and why in one line (audience, moment in the journey, data); a choice without a reason is removed.

## 6. Test, do not guess
Common mistakes: judging one post; changing hook, length and topic at once; chasing a trend that does not fit; a hook the video does not pay off (shown less **[F]**); no next step for the viewer who liked it.
Batches of 8-10 posts, one variable, same format and slot, judge by medians and by the metric the variable should move (hook: 3-s hold; middle: average watch; ending: shares and follows). Weekly review: `playbooks.md` 5. Trends and sounds: only if they fit the mechanism; check business-account licences (`playbooks.md` 4).

## 7. Join with conversion
Reach is not income. Chain: view, follow, profile visit, link or message, purchase; each step has a reason and a metric. Reach and conversion are different jobs; mix plan in `references/virality-to-conversion.md`. Awareness levels (Schwartz): see `copywriting` and `ad-creatives`. Boosting an organic winner as an ad belongs to `ad-creatives` and `paid-traffic`.

## 8. Ethics and law
No fake results, testimonials, scarcity or deadlines; no "guaranteed" income. Paid partnerships and free products must be disclosed with the platform tag and a clear label (Meta 33; FTC 16 CFR 255.5, 36; Brazil CONAR 37). AI-generated or manipulated realistic content: disclose it (EU AI Act art. 50, 35; Meta, TikTok label too). Music: business accounts often need the commercial library or a licence. Do not repost others' videos or watermarked files as your own **[F]**. Not legal advice.

## 9. Output
Match the request: diagnosis (leak, one next test); 3-5 hook options each tagged by mechanism; a timed script (time, spoken line, on-screen text, what the viewer feels); a test batch plan with the metric per variable; a weekly review table; a views-to-sales plan. End with: next action, judging metric, evidence level, what you could not verify.
