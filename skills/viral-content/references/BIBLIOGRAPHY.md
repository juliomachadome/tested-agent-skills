# Bibliography: viral-content

Checked 16/11/2026. Status: **V** = identity matched to the ISBN in OpenLibrary (`https://openlibrary.org/isbn/<isbn>.json`) this pass; **P** = official page opened and the relevant text read this pass; for a peer-reviewed paper **P (identity)** = the DOI resolved in the Crossref registry (`api.crossref.org/works/<doi>`) and title, authors, venue and year matched; the publisher landing page and the full text were not opened, so the finding is restated from the field's well-known result, not re-read; **S** = exists, text not read; **U** = unverified. Nothing is quoted; ideas are restated. Evidence in the skill is tagged [C] consensus or law, [M] serious but single or correlational, [F] practitioner or platform self-report, [X] myth.

## Books
1. Berger, J. *Contagious: Why Things Catch On*. Simon & Schuster, 2013. ISBN 978-1451686579. **V** (the catalogue edition is dated 2016; author Jonah Berger). BOOK by a researcher. Supports: social currency, triggers, emotion, practical value, stories (SKILL.md 2).
2. Heath, C., Heath, D. *Made to Stick*. Random House, 2007. ISBN 978-1400064281. **V** (authors Chip Heath and Dan Heath). BOOK. Supports: unexpectedness, concreteness, credibility, emotion, stories as design checks for memorable ideas.
3. Kahneman, D. *Thinking, Fast and Slow*. Farrar, Straus and Giroux, 2011. ISBN 978-0374533557. **V** (the catalogue edition is dated 2013). BOOK. Supports: fast intuitive processing, familiarity and ease, the idea of experienced vs remembered episodes (peak-end context).

## Peer-reviewed papers: sharing, emotion, diffusion
4. Berger, J., Milkman, K. L. (2012). What makes online content viral? *Journal of Marketing Research* 49(2), 192-205. DOI 10.1509/jmr.10.0353. **P (identity)**. Observational data on news articles: emotion and sharing, high-arousal states, practical utility. Observational and one content type; cited as [M].
5. Berger, J. (2011). Arousal increases social transmission of information. *Psychological Science*. DOI 10.1177/0956797611413294. **P (identity)**. Experiments on arousal and sharing. [M]; see 6 for a later test.
6. Prowten, Walker, London (2024). Does physiological arousal increase social transmission of information. *Psychological Science*. DOI 10.1177/09567976241257255. **P (identity)** from the title match; the finding of this test was **not read**, so the skill says only "later work tests the physiological route; treat arousal as a hypothesis" (**U** for its result).
7. Berger, J. (2014). Word of mouth and interpersonal communication: A review and directions for future research. *Journal of Consumer Psychology* 24(4), 586-607. DOI 10.1016/j.jcps.2014.05.002. **P (identity)**. Review: self-presentation, emotion regulation, information acquisition, social bonding as reasons to share. [M-C]
8. Berger, J., Schwartz, E. M. (2011). What drives immediate and ongoing word of mouth? *Journal of Marketing Research* 48(5), 869-880. DOI 10.1509/jmkr.48.5.869. **P (identity)**. Cues and interest; why some topics keep being talked about. [M]
9. Akpinar, E., Berger, J. (2017). Valuable virality. *Journal of Marketing Research*. DOI 10.1509/jmr.13.0350. **P (identity)**. Emotional appeals and sharing vs brand outcomes. [M]

11. Goel, S., Anderson, A., Hofman, J., Watts, D. J. (2016). The structural virality of online diffusion. *Management Science* 62(1), 180-196. DOI 10.1287/mnsc.2015.2158. **P (identity)**. Most cascades are small, a few are large; different diffusion shapes. [M]
12. Salganik, M. J., Dodds, P. S., Watts, D. J. (2006). Experimental study of inequality and unpredictability in an artificial cultural market. *Science* 311, 854-856. DOI 10.1126/science.1121066. **P (identity)**. Social influence makes outcomes more unequal and less predictable. [M]
13. Vosoughi, S., Roy, D., Aral, S. (2018). The spread of true and false news online. *Science*. DOI 10.1126/science.aap9559. **P (identity)**. Novelty and emotion in spreading; also a warning on false content. [M]

15. Stieglitz, S., Dang-Xuan, L. (2013). Emotions and information diffusion in social media. *Journal of Management Information Systems* 29(4). DOI 10.2753/MIS0742-1222290408. **P (identity)**. Emotionally charged posts spread more and faster in a microblog sample. [M]
