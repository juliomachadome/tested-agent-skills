# From views to sales: the conversion path for organic short video

Reach is attention, not income. The path is: view, follow, profile visit, link click or message, purchase. Each step has a different reason to continue, a different place where people leave, and a metric you can read. Tags: **[C]** consensus or law, **[M]** serious but single or correlational, **[F]** practitioner or platform statement, **[X]** myth. Numbers below are worked examples, not benchmarks; your own history is the baseline.

## 1. The path and what each step needs
| Step | The viewer's question | What it needs | Metric | Fix when it leaks |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| View | Is this for me? | A hook that stops them (mechanism: expectation violation, curiosity) | Hook rate, average watch % | `diagnosis-playbook.md` Trees 2-3 |
| Follow | Do I want more from this person? | A reason: a series with a stated next part, one clear niche, a voice (repeated exposure, parasocial bond; bib. 25, 26) | Follows per 1k views | Tree 4: promise the next part; stay in niche |
| Profile visit | Who is this and what do they offer? | A profile that works as a one-screen landing page | Profile visits per 1k views | Bio in one line: who it is for, the result, the next step; 3 pinned posts that show the best work; a consistent grid |
| Link click or message | Is this worth my next minute? | An offer bridge: a link or DM keyword that matches the viewer's awareness level | Link clicks / profile visits | One link, one promise, wording like the video's own |
| Purchase | Do I trust and need this? | A page and offer that continue the promise (see `copywriting`, `offers`) | Purchases / link clicks | Fix the page and the offer, not the video |
Worked example (10,000 views): 12 follows (1.2 per 1k), 80 profile visits (8 per 1k views), 16 link clicks (20% of visits), 2 purchases (12.5% of clicks). At a price of 40, 2 purchases bring 80 per 10,000 views, which is 8 per 1k views. If you double views and nothing else changes, you double revenue; if you fix the click-to-purchase step from 12.5% to 25% you also double it, without more views. Work on the step where the percentage is weakest relative to your own earlier numbers.

## 2. Content that reaches vs content that converts
| | Reach content | Conversion content |
|---|---|---|
| Job | Stop and share with strangers | Move a relevant person toward the offer |
| Audience | Broad, cold (low awareness) | Narrow, warm (knows the problem or the solution) |
| Mechanism | Surprise, emotion, social currency | Proof, utility, trust, a clear next step |
| Typical result | High views, few follows | Fewer views, higher click and purchase rates |
| Risk | Off-niche audience, no sales | Little reach, no growth |
Mix by rule of thumb **[F]**, to be tested: most posts for reach in the niche, a smaller share for proof (cases, demos, "how it works") and a few direct offers. Look at the follows per 1k and the profile-to-click rate of each type over 10 posts before changing the mix.
Field-study link: nine of the ten videos in `field-study-10-reels.md` are story episodes built for reach; one is an explainer in the first person that tells the audience how the series is made, states reach results, and sells a kit with a stated launch price and date. It is a good example of a conversion piece that keeps the reach piece's participation hook and ends with one plain action. Whether it sold anything is unknown; no sales data was available.

## 3. Match the call to the viewer's awareness level
Awareness levels (unaware, problem-aware, solution-aware, product-aware, most aware; Schwartz) are explained in `skills/copywriting` (`references/decision-psychology.md`, `classics.md`) and `skills/ad-creatives` (`references/concepts-hooks-structure.md`); read them, do not copy. In short for organic video:
- Unaware or problem-aware viewers: show the problem or result and offer a free next step (follow for the next part, a free guide by comment keyword). A price at second 5 loses them.
- Solution-aware: compare approaches, show proof of how yours works, then a low-risk step (a free sample, a call).
- Product-aware: demonstrate, answer the main objection, link to the page.
- Most aware: price, date, plain call.
One video, one level, one call to action.

## 4. Profile as landing page: checklist
- [ ] Name and handle say the niche or result.
- [ ] Bio line: for whom, what they get, the next step; no vague "creator".
- [ ] One link (or a link page with at most 3 items); the same words as the video that sent them.
- [ ] 3 pinned posts: the best proof, the best how-to, the series start.
- [ ] Highlights or playlists organised for a new visitor.
- [ ] DM keyword or lead magnet that matches the viewer's awareness level.
- [ ] Business account contact and any required disclosure (paid partnerships tagged).
Measure: profile visits per 1k views; link clicks per 100 visits; after any change, compare a batch before and after.

## 5. The offer bridge
Say in the video what exists, why it is for this viewer, what it costs or that it is free, and the exact action (link in the bio, comment a word, send a message). Keep the claim the same as on the page. Real deadlines only: a launch price must end on the stated date and then return to the normal price (EU and many national rules treat a fake deadline as unfair; see `skills/offers/references/eu-consumer-law.md` and `skills/copywriting/references/eu-consumer-law.md`). Do not state results you cannot document, and say when a result is a best case.

## 6. Ethics and law for selling in organic content
- Free products, affiliate links and paid partnerships: disclose clearly and use the platform's tag (Meta 33; FTC 16 CFR 255.5, bib. 36; Brazil CONAR arts. 9, 10 and 28, bib. 37).
- Realistic AI-generated people, voices or scenes: disclose (EU AI Act art. 50, bib. 35). Do not present a generated person as a customer.
- No fake scarcity, fake testimonials, fake before-after, or income promises.
- Do not use a sad or frightening story to sell something that does not solve it.
- Handle comments and DMs with care for personal data.

## 7. When to boost an organic winner as an ad
Consider it when a post has beaten your median on the metric tied to the goal over a reasonable sample, has a clear offer or follow reason, and the claims and rights hold up as an advertisement. Organic and paid are different jobs: a paid piece has to be judged by cost per result, not by views, and needs ad-policy and music-licence checks. Rewrite or re-cut it for the ad's goal, then follow `skills/ad-creatives` (creative, hooks and tests) and `skills/paid-traffic` (budget, structure, kill rules). Do not boost a piece whose organic result was a one-off or off-niche.
