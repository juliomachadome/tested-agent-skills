# Playbooks: hooks, scripts, edit, testing, trends, repurposing, review, ethics

Tags: **[C]** consensus or law, **[M]** serious but single, lab or correlational, **[F]** practitioner or platform statement, **[X]** myth. Every hook and template below is a vehicle for a mechanism (defined in `SKILL.md` section 2); pick the mechanism first. A hook that the video does not pay off costs you the viewers it won.

## 1. Hook bank by type (with the mechanism and the honesty limit)
| Type | Mechanism | Pattern (write in your own words) | Honesty limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Result first | Curiosity (how?), proof | Show the finished outcome in the first second, then say what it took | The result must be real and typical, or say it is the best case |
| Pattern interrupt | Expectation violation, motion | An odd image, a sudden change of scene, a counter that moves, a prop that does not belong | It must connect to the topic, not just startle |
| Question | Curiosity gap | The question your viewer already asks themselves, in their words | Answer it; do not ask a question the video never answers |
| Contradiction | Expectation violation, identity | "Everyone does X; here is why Y worked better for me" | Show the evidence or the limit of it |
| Stakes | Arousal, loss | State what is at risk in one concrete sentence | No invented danger or fake emergency |
| Utility | Practical value, saves | "Three steps that fix [specific problem]" | The steps must really work for that case |
| "Watch till the end" | Open loop | Name something that is revealed later | Only if the reveal is real and worth it; if the end disappoints, viewers leave and the post is shown less [F]; never use it as the only hook |
| Story with a participant | Transportation, agency | Put the viewer in the scene ("you wake up and...") and make the next choice theirs | One protagonist, one problem; keep the stakes consistent |
The field study (`field-study-10-reels.md`) shows the last row in a serial form: a moving counter, an impossible premise stated in a sentence, a recap of what viewers chose before, a forced choice at the end. The mechanism is expectation violation plus agency, not the counter itself.
Hook checklist: it can be understood with sound off; there is one subject; there is movement or a face in frame 1; the first sentence has a concrete noun or number; the promise matches the caption; it works for your niche without a platform trend.

## 2. Script templates (timed; adjust length to the idea)
**A. Problem, process, reveal** (15-45 s). 0-3 s: the problem in the viewer's words, or the result. 3-10 s: why it matters (stakes). 10-30 s: the process in 3 steps, concrete. Final 5 s: the reveal and one next step. Mechanism: curiosity, utility.
**B. Before, after** (10-30 s). 0-2 s: the after. 2-8 s: the before. 8-25 s: what changed and how. End: a line the viewer can pass on. Mechanism: contrast, social currency.
**C. List** (20-45 s). Hook promises a count. Put the second-best item first, the best last (peak-end, bib. 23). Cut any item that is filler. Mechanism: utility, saves.
**D. Story with stakes** (40-120 s). Set the situation and the person in 5 s; state what could be lost; escalate twice; place the twist at 60-85% of the length; end on the choice or the consequence. Mechanism: transportation, curiosity, peak-end.
**E. Serial episode** (60-120 s). Recap what happened or what viewers decided (0-15 s); resolve one question; open one new question before the end; end with a clear next step. Promise a cadence you can keep. Mechanism: series promise, repeated exposure.
Table to use for any script: Time | Spoken line | On screen (text) | What the viewer feels or wonders | Mechanism.

## 3. Editing checklist
- [ ] First frame is the strongest still; nothing before the idea.
- [ ] Hook visible and audible by 1 s; sound off still works (burned-in captions, large, inside the safe zone).
- [ ] A change (cut, zoom, caption, angle) at least every few seconds; the right rate depends on the style: in 10 viral videos it ranged from 2 to 5 scene changes per 10 s, so do not copy a number; test your own.
- [ ] The slowest 3 seconds removed.
- [ ] Captions edited by hand; keywords emphasised; no wall of text.
- [ ] Sound: clear voice above music; original, licensed or platform-library music.
- [ ] The ending is the strongest beat; no "thanks for watching" dead air.
- [ ] Length is the shortest that keeps the payoff. Shorts up to 3 minutes are allowed (bib. 32); a longer video must keep a pending question.
- [ ] Safe zones: keep text away from the bottom and the sides where buttons sit; check the preview.
- [ ] Cover frame and caption written; one next step.

## 4. Trends, sounds and rights
Use a trend only when it fits the mechanism and your audience; late trends bring viewers who do not match your niche (see Tree 4 in the diagnosis playbook). Check the sound's availability for business accounts: commercial-use libraries and licences differ from personal accounts. Do not use copyrighted tracks, films or other creators' clips without permission; "credit in the caption" is not a licence **[C]**. Remaking an idea is fine; copying a file is not. Keep a credits file with author, link and licence for every asset.

## 5. Posting and testing routine
- **Batches**: 8-10 posts per test, same format and slot, one variable (hook type, length, ending, topic). More than one change makes the result unreadable.
- **Cadence**: the sources read here give no required number (bib. 29-32). Choose the volume you can keep for 8 weeks, and test it as a variable; do not post filler to hit a number.
- **Comparison**: compare medians of the metric the variable should move (hook: 3-s hold; middle: average watch %; end: shares and follows per 1k). A difference smaller than the spread within a group is noise; with under 10 posts per arm, call it a lead, not a result.
- **Post time**: record it; keep the slot fixed within a batch.
- **Log**: a table with date, length, hook type, mechanism, 3-s hold, average watch %, shares and saves per 1k, follows per 1k, notes.
- **Winners**: write the mechanism in a sentence and make 5 variants; do not clone the file.
Worked example: variants A (result-first hook) and B (question hook), 8 posts each. Medians of 3-s hold: A 46%, B 39%; best-to-worst spread within A is 12 points. The 7-point gap is smaller than the spread: keep testing before choosing.

## 6. Repurposing across platforms
Export the original file without any platform watermark; do not reuse a clip that carries another app's mark (recommenders may limit it, and it signals recycled content **[F]**; check the current rule). Re-cut for the native shape: caption style, safe zones, length, sound, the first frame. Different captions and on-screen text per platform. Post natively; do not link-share between them. Check that the hook still fits the audience on each (Reels, TikTok, Shorts differ in who is there and in how views are counted, bib. 29, 30, 32).

## 7. Weekly review agenda (30 minutes)
1. Pull the last 7 days and the last 10-20 posts; compute medians (not means). 2. Which post beat the median by 2x or more, which fell below half? 3. For each, name the mechanism that was present or missing. 4. Where did viewers drop (3 s, middle, end)? 5. Shares, saves and follows per 1k by post. 6. Profile visits and link clicks if relevant. 7. Next week's one variable and the 8-10 posts that will test it. 8. One thing to stop.
Output table: post | length | hook type | mechanism | 3-s hold | avg watch % | shares/1k | follows/1k | verdict.

## 8. Ethics and law
- Claims: no invented results, no fake customers, no "guaranteed income", no health or finance promises you cannot document. Real deadlines only.
- Disclosure: paid partnerships, gifts and affiliate links must be disclosed clearly and use the platform's tag (Meta 33). US rule: a connection the audience does not expect must be disclosed clearly and conspicuously (FTC, 16 CFR 255.5, bib. 36). Brazil: advertising must be identifiable and indirect advertising follows the code (CONAR arts. 9, 10 and 28, bib. 37). Not legal advice; local counsel for campaigns.
- AI-generated content: if a realistic person, voice, place or event is generated or manipulated, disclose it; the EU AI Act sets duties on deployers of deep fakes and on providers marking outputs (art. 50, bib. 35). Apply the platform's own AI label where it exists (policy not verified this pass). A synthetic narrator does not need a fake persona; do not present generated people as real customers or experts.
- Originality: do not post other creators' videos as your own; do not remove their marks.
- Music: licensed or library only for business accounts.
- Audience: do not target minors with deceptive hooks; do not provoke fear or outrage for engagement on topics where harm is possible.
- Data: do not collect personal data in comments or DMs without a lawful basis (check the privacy law of the data subject's country).

## Field-tested additions (16/11/2026)
- **Second person, present tense.** Lyrics with more "you" were liked and bought more, with experiments behind the archive data (Packard & Berger 2020, bib. 38); self-referencing raises transportation (Escalas 2007, bib. 39); "you" in narratives shifts readers to the actor's perspective (Brunyé et al., bib. 40) **[M]**. Use it when the viewer can plausibly be the protagonist; it is a lever, not a rule for every first line.
- **Sends per reach.** Trade press reports Instagram's head naming watch time, likes per reach and sends (DM shares) per reach as the main Reels signals in January 2025 (secondary sources, weights unpublished) **[F]**. Design for a reason to send: a question that names a friend's choice ("what would you do?"), a dilemma, a useful tip.
- **Vote, then show the result.** End on a real A/B choice; open the next part with the share that chose each option and follow the majority for real. Comments become a one-letter vote (cheap to give) and the recap rewards voters (social proof) **[F, inference]**. Comments are then inflated by design: pick winners by retention, sends and follows per 1k views, not by comment count.
