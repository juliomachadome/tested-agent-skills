# GEO: appearing and being cited in AI answers

## How it works (official)
- **[C]** AI Overviews and AI Mode use search grounding: normal ranking retrieves pages from the index and the model answers from them, using **query fan-out** ("a set of concurrent, related queries generated by the model"). Being a good result for the topic's **sub-questions** counts, not only the head query. (Google AI optimisation guide, 15/05/2026)
- **[C]** "Optimizing for generative AI search is optimizing for the search experience, and thus still SEO."
- **[C] What Google recommends:** non-commodity content with expert or lived insight "beyond common knowledge"; visitor focus; technical basics; relevant images and video next to text.
- **[C] What Google says is not needed:** machine-readable "AI text files", AI-specific markup or Markdown; chopping text into tiny pieces; writing specially for AI; covering every long-tail variant; special schema. In inflected languages this includes not repeating every form of the keyword.
- **[C] Bing (Feb 2026)** names GEO in its guidelines and lists what makes content eligible for Copilot grounding and citation: facts stated directly, clear consistent entity names, **one URL = one topic**, essential information near the top. Citation is not guaranteed.

## The GEO study (Aggarwal et al., KDD 2024) [M]
- Benchmark of 10k queries (~80% informational), measuring a source's "visibility" in generated answers (position-adjusted word count, baseline 19.3): **Quotation Addition +41%**, **Statistics Addition +31%**, **Fluency Optimization +28%**, **Cite Sources +27%**, Technical Terms +18%, Easy-to-Understand +14%, Authoritative tone +10%, Unique Words +6%, **Keyword Stuffing −8%**.
- On live Perplexity (200 queries): quotations +21-22%; statistics +9% (word metric) and +37% (subjective impression); stuffing ~9% worse.
- Gains are much larger for lower-ranked sources: "Cite Sources" gave +115% to the 5th-position source and −30% to the 1st. Good news for small sites.
- Varies by domain: statistics for law/government and debate; citing sources for factual claims; quotations for people/society, explanation, history; fluency for business, science, health. Best combo: fluency + statistics.
- Caveats: two engines (one simulated on GPT-3.5 over 5 results, plus Perplexity), engines change, classic ranking not measured, one source rewritten with the rest fixed. Practical translation: **fact with a number, a named source and a clear sentence**; invented numbers are deception.

## What engines cite (vendor studies)
- **[M]** AI Overviews (one SEO-tool vendor, Mar 2026; 863k SERPs, 4M URLs; country not stated): 37.9% of cited URLs were in the same query's top 10; 31.2% in 11-100; 31.0% outside the top 100. In Jul 2025 it was ~76% with a different method. YouTube: 5.6% of all cited URLs and 18.2% of those not ranking in the top 100.
- **[M]** Outside the US the pattern can be more conservative: in one EU-market study (Aug 2025), average organic position of cited sources 6.7; 59% of citations from the top 10; 96% from local-language sources. Find the study for the project's market; absent one, assume ranking in the local language remains the main path. [1-SRC per market]
- **[M]** Brand web mentions correlate most with AI Overview visibility (Spearman 0.66), ahead of branded anchors (0.53), branded search volume (0.39), domain rating (0.33) and backlinks (0.22); 26% of brands had zero mentions. **Correlation, not cause.** Bought "mentions" do not help.
- **[F]** AI engines prefer earned media over brand content more than Google does (preprint, read via summaries).
- **[M]/[F]** ChatGPT citations correlate with referring domains and text length in one vendor study; another, controlling for authority, found no effect. Not a length rule.
- **[C]** Copilot uses the Bing index. **[F]** ChatGPT's result sources are undocumented (OpenAI mentioned a set of services where Bing matters; vendors report its own index and Google results). Certain: allow `OAI-SearchBot`. Being in Bing (Webmaster Tools + IndexNow) stays cheap and useful.

## Traffic: citation is not a click
- **[M]** US survey panel (Mar 2025): clicks on traditional results in 8% of visits with an AI summary vs 15% without; clicks on links inside the summary 1%; 18% of searches had a summary. A vendor model (desktop, Dec 2023 vs Dec 2025): position-1 CTR ~58% lower with an AI Overview. A German study (2025): first-link CTR from 27% to 11%. Google disputes these methods and says remaining clicks are higher quality.
- **[M]** One EU-market study (Jun 2025 vs Jun 2024, ~1.4k domains): clicks −19% with impressions +3%; non-brand queries −29%, brand −9%. Brand protects clicks. [1-SRC]
- **[F]** "AI visitors convert 4.4× better": partially published method, no replication. Measure in your own funnel.

## llms.txt: real status
- **[C]** Proposal (Sep 2024; v2 Aug 2026): Markdown at `/llms.txt` with H1, summary and link lists, meant for on-demand agent use, not training.
- **[C]** Google Search does not use it (said in 2025; written in the AI guide 15/06/2026: not needed). A Lighthouse experimental "Agentic Browsing" audit (May 2026) checks it: agent readiness, not a search signal.
- **[M]** In ~300k domains, ~10% have the file; no correlation with citation frequency.
- Default: if it exists and is auto-generated, keep it; if not, do not create it for SEO. Never count it as a GEO tactic or put content in it that differs from the pages.

## AI crawlers and controls
- **[C] OpenAI:** `OAI-SearchBot` decides presence in ChatGPT search ("sites opted out will not be shown"); `GPTBot` is training; `ChatGPT-User` is user-initiated and may ignore robots.txt. Robots changes take ~24 h.
- **[C] Perplexity:** allow `PerplexityBot` to appear; `Perplexity-User` is user-initiated and usually ignores robots. **Anthropic:** `Claude-SearchBot` (search index), `Claude-User` (on request), `ClaudeBot` (training). Search and training are separate decisions: allow search, deny training if desired.
- **[C] Google:** AI Overviews and AI Mode use Googlebot; `Google-Extended` controls training and grounding outside Search and does not affect Search. Since 2026 Search Console has a site-level "Search generative AI" control: off removes the site from AI Overviews, AI Mode and Discover AI while keeping normal results (effect details from third-party coverage). On by default; turn off only by explicit owner decision.
- **[C]** `nosnippet`, `data-nosnippet`, `max-snippet` and `noindex` limit what appears in Google's AI features; never on content you want cited.
- **[C] Preferred sources:** users choose sites to see more of in Top Stories and, since May 2026, in AI Overviews/AI Mode; mainly matters for news publishers.
- **[M]** Check server/CDN logs for which AI bots really visit and what status they get (404s and firewall blocks are common). A default "block AI bots" firewall rule overrides robots.txt.
