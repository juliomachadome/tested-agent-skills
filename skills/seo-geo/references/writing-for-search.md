# Writing for search (people first, engines second)

Final prose is the writer's job; this file is the brief and the checks. Tags as in SKILL.md.

## 1. Search intent types
| Intent | Reader wants | Page type | Opening |
|---|---|---|---|
| Informational | to know or learn | guide, explainer, definition | direct answer in the first sentence, then depth |
| Navigational | a specific site or page | clear brand/product page | name, official link, key action |
| Commercial investigation | to compare before buying | comparison, review, alternatives | criteria first, honest trade-offs |
| Transactional | to do or buy now | product or sign-up page | the offer, price, next step |
| Local | something nearby | local page, profile | place, hours, how to reach |
Read the live results to confirm: the page types already ranking show what the engine believes the intent is [C]. One intent, one URL.

## 2. Helpful content and E-E-A-T
- **[C]** Google's helpful-content guidance asks: original information, a complete description, insight beyond the obvious, satisfying the reader so they need not search again, and a clear who, how, why.
- **[C]** E-E-A-T (experience, expertise, authoritativeness, trust) comes from the Search Quality Rater Guidelines; raters use it to judge quality and Google uses their ratings to evaluate systems; it is not one ranking factor. Trust is the center. YMYL topics (health, money, safety, civic) get the highest bar.
- Show experience with real assets: your own data, screenshots, photos, steps tested, named author with real credentials, editorial policy, dated updates with what changed.

## 3. Answer-first structure (what AI engines quote)
1. Heading in the user's own words; the answer in the first 1-2 sentences, complete without context (40-60 words is a practitioner rule [F]).
2. Then the reasoning: steps, numbers with named linked sources, an example.
3. One idea per paragraph; lists and tables for steps and comparisons; definitions stated plainly.
4. Name entities in full once (product, place, standard, person) and use the same name afterwards.
5. FAQ only for real questions from search data or support; not as filler.
Vendor evidence (Ahrefs, Semrush and similar studies on what AI Overviews cite) shows overlap with top organic pages and a preference for clear, structured answers; the samples and methods differ, so use as hypothesis [M].

## 4. Headings, entities, internal links
- H1 states the topic; H2s are the questions or steps a reader asks; do not skip levels; no keyword-stuffed headings.
- Entities: cover the people, products, terms and relations a subject expert would; link to primary sources; use the Organization and author pages for identity.
- Internal links: 2-4 contextual links in the body with descriptive anchors; link from older related pages to the new one; a hub page per topic cluster. Orphan pages rarely perform [M].

## 5. Readability
Sentences 12-20 words, plain verbs, short paragraphs on mobile, defined jargon, no walls of text. Readability scores are a screen, not a goal; tools differ by language (see copywriting `transcreation-readability.md`).

## 6. Titles and meta descriptions
- **[C]** Title: unique, describes the page, main phrase near the start, brand at the end; Google may rewrite titles and snippets. About 50-60 characters is a display rule of thumb, not a limit [F].
- **[C]** Meta description: a faithful summary that earns the click, not a ranking factor; about 120-155 characters displayed; Google often builds its own snippet from the page.
- Write for the reader; do not promise what the page does not deliver; no clickbait.

## 7. Content brief template
```
Working title / URL slug
Search intent and the live-SERP page types
Audience, country, language
Primary question and 3-8 related questions (autocomplete, PAA, Search Console, support)
What the top 10 lacks: our own data, asset or experience
Answer-first opening (draft sentence)
Outline: H2s with the evidence each needs
Sources to cite (primary, with links)
Author or reviewer, and why credible
Internal links in / out
Funnel stage, next step, one CTA
Schema type if any (matches visible content)
Success metric and read dates (4 and 8 weeks)
```

## 8. Refresh cadence and content decay
- **[M]** Pages lose clicks as facts age and competitors publish; vendor datasets show decay is common but timing differs by topic.
- Review: fast-moving or YMYL pages every 3-6 months; evergreen every 12 months; trigger on a drop in clicks or position in Search Console for 4+ weeks, or on a law, price or product change.
- Update substantially (facts, examples, structure), then change the visible date and `lastmod`. A date bump without edits is a search-first signal. Merge or redirect pages that overlap; prune pages that serve nobody (410 or 301).

## 9. What not to do
Scaled content with no added value (AI or human), spun or translated-only pages, one page per city or keyword variant, fake authors or reviews, invented statistics, text meant to game AI citation, expired-domain tactics, content parasites on a strong host (site-reputation abuse). See the spam list in SKILL.md and Google's spam policies. AI-assisted writing is allowed when accountable humans verify facts and the page helps the reader.
