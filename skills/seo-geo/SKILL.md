# SEO and GEO

Optimising for AI search is still SEO: AI Overviews, AI Mode and Copilot answer from pages in the normal index. What wins is useful, original, trustworthy content on a technically clean site, with direct facts, named sources and clear entities. No special schema, file or style "for AI".

Evidence tags: **[C]** official search-engine docs, or docs and research agree · **[M]** large-sample study with published method, but correlational/vendor/one moment · **[F]** small sample, unpublished method, second-hand, practitioner · **[X]** contradicted by docs or data. **[1-SRC]** single source. Every rule carries its source; if older than 6 months and the decision is expensive, reopen it (`references/BIBLIOGRAPHY.md`).

## Core principles

1. **People-first test:** would someone arriving directly, without search, find the page useful? Content made mainly to attract search visits is a warning sign. Ask: original information or analysis? More than a rewrite of sources? Factual errors? Does the reader leave satisfied? [C]
2. **No preferred word count.** Length = what the question needs. [C]
3. **Dates change only with substantial change.** `dateModified` and `lastmod` move only with real edits. [C]
4. **Trust is the most important part of E-E-A-T;** E-E-A-T is not a single ranking factor. Show first-hand experience with real assets (screens, data, photos). [C]
5. **Who, how, why:** clear authorship where readers expect it; disclose AI where a reader would wonder. **An invented author (AI headshot, made-up name, false credentials) is deception** and makes the page untrustworthy; a brand persona never signs as a person or becomes a `Person` in schema. An organisation as author is fine. [C]
6. **One intent, one URL.** Two pages for the same intent compete; a site usually gets ~2 results. Merge and 301. [C]
7. **AI writing is allowed; scale without value is not.** Fact-check everything generated by hand, including titles, meta descriptions, schema and alt text; real editorial review by someone accountable. Scaled content abuse applies to hand-written text too. [C]
8. **Indexed + snippet-eligible is all AI features need.** Allow crawling (robots, CDN, firewall), keep content in text, link to the page internally. [C]
9. **Server-rendered HTML for everything indexable.** Google renders JavaScript in a queue; many AI crawlers (late 2024) did not run it. [C][M]
10. **Facts with numbers and named sources** help readers and AI citation; invented statistics are deception. Citation is not a click: measure product actions. Only Search Console and Bing Webmaster hold first-party data. [M]

## Never do (search-engine spam policies) [C]
Scaled content abuse (many low-value pages, AI or not; scraped, spun or machine-translated; one template swapping a word) · low-effort copied/paraphrased main content even with credit · doorway pages (one page per city for an unchanged service) · third-party content riding site signals · expired-domain abuse · thin affiliate pages · keyword stuffing, hidden text, cloaking, sneaky redirects, link schemes (paid links carry `sponsored`/`nofollow`) · misleading functionality (a "test" that only captures e-mail) · fabricated or incentivised reviews in `Review`/`AggregateRating` · back-button hijacking (spam policy since Apr 2026) · prompt injection or text engineered to trigger AI citation (Bing, Feb 2026) · buying "mentions". Accordions and tabs are not hidden text.

## Workflow for a content page

1. Project first (above), then gather facts (product, audience, country, language, next funnel step, who signs, existing pages per intent, how metadata/JSON-LD/sitemap are produced). Missing: ask once, assume aloud.
2. Query research in the audience's language (autocomplete with the market's `hl`/`gl`, PAA, Search Console); volume only from real sources.
3. Brief: intent, funnel stage, business potential (0-3), the gap the top 10 lacks (own data, product asset, this audience's typical error), sources, internal links in/out.
4. Writer drafts; facts checked at source; native-level language review.
5. Technical and pre-publish checklist below.
6. Publish: URL Inspection, IndexNow for Bing; note the date; read at 4 and 8 weeks.

## AI tells to avoid (check while creating, not after)
- Thin pages that restate the top results with nothing new; fix: first-hand evidence, original numbers, a named expert.
- "Ultimate guide" or "Everything you need to know" titles; name the one thing the page settles.
- Keyword stuffing and repeated exact-match phrases; write for one search intent per page.
- FAQ blocks invented to rank; keep only questions real users ask (Search Console, support, PAA).
- No first-hand experience or original data: add the screenshot, measurement or test only you have.
- Generic meta descriptions that fit any page; state the concrete answer or benefit of this one.
- Mass-produced location or comparison pages from one template: scaled content abuse in Google's spam policies.
- Text tells: em-dash overuse, "unlock/elevate/seamless", rule of three, empty openers, vague attributions ("experts say"), hedging filler.
Say what you chose and why in one line; a choice without a reason is removed. If a humanizer skill is installed, run it as a final pass on the finished copy, then re-check facts and numbers it may have touched; never depend on it.

## Pre-publish checklist

Content and trust
- [ ] Answers a real question in the audience's language (autocomplete, PAA, Search Console).
- [ ] Has something the top 10 lacks (product asset, own analysis).
- [ ] Direct answer at the top: the question answered in the first sentence with a verifiable fact.
- [ ] Every number has a named, linked, preferably primary source.
- [ ] True authorship (real person or organisation); no persona as a person; link to the editorial policy.

Technical
- [ ] Initial HTML contains the text, prerendered when possible; indexable; in the sitemap with real `lastmod`; own absolute canonical.
- [ ] Unique clear title, unique description, readable URL with hyphens.
- [ ] 2-4 internal links with descriptive anchors; one older page links here.
- [ ] JSON-LD matches the visible content and validates; no "AI schema".
- [ ] LCP, INP and CLS in "good".
- [ ] No `noarchive`/`nosnippet`; AI search crawlers allowed; alt on images; official sources for YMYL; Next.js canonical and metadata in initial HTML.

Conversion and measurement
- [ ] One call to action tied to the topic, to the next funnel step.
- [ ] Published: URL Inspection + IndexNow; 4- and 8-week reads dated.
