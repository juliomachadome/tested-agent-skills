# Playbooks: market research

Templates to copy and fill. Write the threshold before you look at results. Tags: **[C]** consensus, **[F]** practitioner.

## 1. The 48-hour validation sprint
Goal: one behavioural signal from strangers in two days, at near-zero cost. Works for any product type; for a physical product the "page" shows a photo or render and a pre-order.

**Before (1 hour).** Fill: segment (one sentence), problem and moment, alternative today, promise, price to test, thresholds, kill rule. Example thresholds: from 500 qualified visits, at least 30 qualified signups (6 percent) and at least 5 pre-sales or deposits; below 10 signups is no-go for this segment.
**Day 1 morning.** Research: 30 minutes on Trends (BR and world, 5 years, problem terms), 30 minutes reading communities and marketplace reviews (copy raw phrases, tag themes), 20 minutes listing 5 alternatives (`playbooks.md` section 2).
**Day 1 afternoon.** Build the page: headline with the promise, 3 bullets of outcome, price or price range, honest delivery date, one qualifying question ("what do you do today about X"), an email field, consent line and privacy link. No fake scarcity, no invented testimonials.
**Day 1 evening to Day 2.** Send traffic to strangers who match: 3-5 relevant communities where self-promo is allowed, a small paid test (R$ 150-300 or the local equivalent; set budget and kill rule up front, see `paid-traffic`), direct messages only with a lawful basis.
**Day 2 end.** Read: visits, signup rate, answers to the qualifier, pre-sales, replies. Decide with the scorecard (section 4): go small, pivot (which part: segment, problem, price, format) or no-go. Write what could not be learned in 48 hours (retention, delivery cost, long-run demand).

## 2. Competitor teardown template
One row per alternative; include "do nothing" and the free option.

| Field | Fill with |
|---|---|
| Name, link, type | direct / indirect / substitute / do nothing |
| Offer and format | what is sold, deliverables, length, support |
| Price and plans | list, discounts, trials, guarantee (note date seen) |
| Promise and proof | headline, claimed outcomes, evidence shown |
| Audience and positioning | who it says it is for, what it claims to beat |
| Channels | where it gets traffic: search, social, ads (ad libraries), marketplace, partners |
| Traction | review count and recency, rank, ad age, hiring, community size; mark estimates as estimates |
| Review themes | top 3 praised phrases, top 3 complaints, "I wish" requests |
| Weakness and gap | what customers pay for that is missing |

Review mining **[F]**: read 30 reviews at 1-2 stars (complaints), 30 at 5 stars (the words to reuse), and every "wish it had" line; cluster into themes and count. Marketplace evidence in Brazil (Hotmart, Kiwify, Mercado Livre): ranking, price, rating and number of reviews show relative popularity, not revenue; say so. Output: a one-paragraph position ("for X who Y, unlike Z, we ..."), the gap, and the evidence that customers pay to close it.

## 3. Willingness-to-pay survey template
Use after a problem is validated, with people who have it. Sample: 100 or more per segment for a stable curve, 30 for a rough bracket; say which. Tell respondents the purpose, what you store and how to opt out; consent before collecting contact data.
1. Screening: "In the last 3 months did you do X?" (exclude others).
2. Problem: "What did you do about it, and what did it cost you in money or hours?"
3. Van Westendorp (four questions about this product, described in one paragraph): too cheap to trust its quality; a bargain; getting expensive but you would still consider it; too expensive to consider. Ask all four per respondent, in a sensible order.
4. Gabor-Granger: show one randomly chosen price step; "Would you buy at this price?" yes or no; if yes, show the next higher, else the next lower (steps like 47, 67, 97, 147).
5. Commitment: "Join the pre-order list with a refundable deposit?" (stronger than any stated answer).
6. Segments: role, budget holder, current spend.
Analysis and caveats: `sizing-and-wtp.md`. Stated WTP overstates; use the range to choose pre-sale prices, then test them.

## 4. Decision scorecard with worked example
Score each 0-5 with evidence written next to the score. Weights default to 100 points; change them with a stated reason.

| Criterion | Weight | What a 5 looks like | What a 0 looks like |
|---|---|---|---|
| Demand | 20 | Strangers ask repeatedly and pay for workarounds | No asks outside own audience |
| Willingness to pay | 20 | Strangers paid at or above target price | Everyone declines when priced |
| Reachability | 15 | A known channel with affordable cost per visit | No way to reach the segment |
| Competition and differentiation | 10 | Proven market plus a paid-for gap | Dominant free option, no gap |
| Effort and cost | 10 | Ships in days, margin clear | Months, margin unclear |
| Window and timing | 10 | Evergreen or window fits delivery | Window closes before delivery |
| Risk and compliance | 5 (5 = low risk) | No legal, platform or reputation exposure | Probable legal or platform block |
| Founder fit | 10 | Credibility, skill and interest | None |

Total = sum of weight x score / 5. Rules: a 0 on demand, willingness to pay or reachability caps the call at pivot or no-go; below 55 no-go; 55-70 pivot or test more; above 70 go small. A small test is part of "go".

Worked example (idea: a spreadsheet and video kit for freelance designers to price projects). Scores: demand 4, willingness to pay 3, reachability 4, competition 3, effort 4, timing 2 (no urgency), risk 4, fit 5. Points: 16 + 12 + 12 + 6 + 8 + 4 + 4 + 10 = 72. Call: go small. Weakest items: willingness to pay (only 6 strangers paid, so directional) and timing. Next test: pre-sale to 150 qualified visitors at two prices; threshold 8 buyers; stop if fewer than 3.

## 5. Pre-sale plan with refund terms
1. **Offer**: what is delivered, when (a firm date with slack), format, and what is not included. Describe the real state ("in production, first module on DATE").
2. **Price and capacity**: a price from the WTP bracket (price design: `offers`); a real cap if capacity is limited; no invented scarcity.
3. **Refund terms**: a stated full refund if you do not deliver by DATE or if the first deliverable does not match the description, and the legal right of withdrawal where it applies (EU and Brazil have statutory rights for distance sales; check local law). Refund through the same payment method within the stated days.
4. **Payment**: use a processor that supports refunds; do not hold money without a process; keep records.
5. **Communication**: confirmation email with the date, the refund route and a contact; progress update at the half-way point; ship a small first piece early.
6. **Thresholds**: minimum buyers to build (say 8 or 10), minimum revenue to cover production, deadline. If missed, refund everyone and tell them.
7. **After**: ask buyers why they bought and what almost stopped them; this becomes the copy and the roadmap.
