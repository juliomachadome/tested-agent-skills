# Quiz funnels and low-ticket playbook

**Evidence warning.** The low-ticket material here rests mainly on **one source**: a long 2026 practitioner podcast by Brazilian low-ticket operators (Meta ads, instant payments, info-products). Everything from it is **[F][1-SRC]**: practitioner reports with survivorship bias, no controlled data, a non-EU market. Quiz-pattern observations come from public funnel teardowns, almost all written by vendors of funnel tools, whose screen counts disagree with each other **[F]**. Several tactics they report are **illegal in the EU**; they are listed in the last section so they can be recognised and refused.

## Principles worth testing
1. **Offer first, product second.** Validate the offer with a minimum deliverable before producing the full product; the deliverable barely moves front conversion but drives refunds, success and backend sales.
2. **Many cheap attempts.** Validation was reported at a few hundred currency units per offer; one hit pays for several misses. Kill an offer that still breaks even after new creatives and variations: "good offers are born good".
3. **Research is the copy.** Most of the copywriter's time goes to audience, market and mechanism research; the copy is assembled from it.
4. **Simplicity wins.** Plain pages and plain video repeatedly beat "professional" redesigns in their tests; gamifying a quiz did not save a weak idea.
5. **Follow the market's format, innovate on the idea.** Copy the format, question count, ticket and deliverable type of the top offers in the niche; innovate only through A/B tests after validation.
6. **Do not give away the whole method in the front offer.** Sell one pillar; leave room for the backend.
7. **Profit is the metric.** Decide by whole-funnel profit (front + bump + upsell), never by CTR, engagement or retention alone; a headline with more interaction lost to one that sold more.

## Mechanism, name and "one belief"
- **Mechanism** is a proof element: why it works. In low ticket, the solution mechanism alone tends to work better than also explaining the problem mechanism, and it is shown openly in the ad.
- **Unique mechanism** = an existing approach given a new name and narrative, to restore hope for people who already tried. Must stay truthful.
- **Name test ("dry test"):** 10-20 candidate names from 3-5 mechanisms; for each name, the same funnel (several image ads → quiz → checkout) with only the name changed; measure by purchases, CPC only as a signal. The winning name defines the mechanism. Naming criteria: easy to say and retell, currently talked about, curious, plus credible and emotionally relevant (the "made to stick" criteria). For sceptical audiences, credibility outweighs hype.
- **One belief:** "[new opportunity] is the key to [desire], and that is only possible with [mechanism]." Used in every ad, quiz step and backend message. Must be phrased so it is not an unprovable outcome promise ("stays longer in memory" beats "you will be fluent").
- **Awareness and sophistication** (Schwartz) decide how much explanation the first step needs: a "free test" works only if the audience already knows the topic.

## Quiz structure
**Step 1 (the 80/20 of the funnel):** direct promise + mechanism name as headline; an image of the product or result; a personalisation button ("get my personalised plan", never "start quiz"); everything above the fold on the smallest phone. Ask only for the first "yes". Questions start at step 2.

**Middle** (common pattern):
1. Situation questions first, not pain (warm-up).
2. Break 1 after one or two questions: who this is for, why finish, the one belief, the strongest real proof.
3. Problem, implication and need questions (SPIN-like), so the person reaches the conclusion.
4. Breaks against click-fatigue: real testimonial, explanatory loading screen.
5. Optional mini video on mechanism and credibility (only with a real, verifiable expert).
6. Pre-pitch: a commitment question ("will you do 5 minutes a day this week?"), every answer continues, none is punished.
7. Offer block or short offer video.

**Transition to the offer:** a loading screen that repeats the person's answers, then a plan named with their data and a chart that starts at their answer. In the EU: no target dates of outcome on the chart ("fluent by March") without proof.

**Patterns seen across category leaders [F]:** goal before pain; a reactive line commenting the previous answer (zero extra clicks if shown atop the next question); growing micro-commitments reused later; e-mail asked late (~90% of the way) and sold as "see my results", with marketing consent separate; the paywall continuing the quiz's language and chart; length is less of a problem than boredom ("there is no long funnel, only a boring one"), but long quizzes need a ticket that pays for 10 minutes of attention.

**Test order inside a quiz [F][1-SRC]:** headline → image → button text → page layout imitating content the audience consumes. One test at a time, 50/50, measured by sales.

## Video sales letters
- Pitch length depends on niche and ticket: one operator reported short post-pitch working for very low tickets; another reported +20% from a long post-pitch on a long VSL. Test it. [1-SRC]
- Each post-pitch element (a testimonial, an objection answered) must add; if conversion drops, the elements are wrong or have become hammering.

## Practitioner tactics that are illegal or unsafe in the EU (recognise and refuse)
| Tactic | Why | Alternative |
|---|---|---|
| "Only 10 minutes", resetting countdowns, "you can only answer once" | False urgency (UCPD Annex I no. 7) | Real dated deadline |
| Back-button hijacking, exit traps | Manipulative pattern; also a search-engine spam policy | Normal back; exit offer that only reminds of the free step |
| Pause/exit coupon of 20-30% | New 30-day lowest price; trains abandonment | No coupon; real dated price |
| Inflated anchor ("Ferrari at the price of a beetle") | Fictitious reduction | Honest, dated comparison |
| Seat or access scarcity on digital products | No real scarcity | Nothing |
| Recurring fabricated reasons for discounts ("celebrating N followers") | Fabricated reason; each reduction needs the 30-day line | No reduction below current price |
| "Abuse" testimonials; filtered reviews | Fake or unverified reviews banned (Annex I 23b-c) | Invite every buyer after the guarantee window; show how reviews are verified |
| AI expert or persona shown as a real person; undisclosed synthetic voice | Misleading; AI Act art. 50 transparency | Real person with contract, or a brand/team voice; disclose synthetic media |
| Upsell disguised as onboarding | Offer must be recognisable | Video that presents itself as an offer with price and pay button |
| Messaging-app/SMS recovery without opt-in; reusing checkout leads for marketing | ePrivacy/GDPR purpose limitation | Transactional e-mail without promotion; marketing only with separate consent |
| Token first month with hard cancellation | Subscription sweep target | Prepaid without renewal, or cancel in as many clicks as signup |
| Outcome promises with deadlines | Unproven claim | Describe what the person does and gets |
| Re-uploading someone else's viral video with a new CTA | Copyright and image rights | Recreate the idea with own material |
| Price varying by profile in a price test | Personalised price needs disclosure | Sequential periods, same price for all |
