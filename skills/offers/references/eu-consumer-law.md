# EU consumer law for offers, prices and guarantees

## 1. Price-reduction announcements
- Any announced reduction states the "prior price" = the **lowest price applied in the 30 days before the reduction** (art. 6a(1)-(2)).
- Counts as a reduction announcement (Guidance 1.1): % or amount off; new price next to a struck-through price; "sale", "special offer", "Black Friday" when they suggest a reduction; **presenting the current price as a "launch price" against a higher future normal price**.
- The % and "you save" are computed on the 30-day lowest price, not the full price; showing the lowest price in small print while computing on another value does not comply (C-330/23).
- Outside art. 6a but still under the UCPD (Guidance 1.1, 2.3, 3): general claims ("best prices"); comparison with a competitor or recommended retail price that does not look like a reduction; conditional offers ("3 for 2"); loyalty schemes and genuinely personal reductions (birthday coupon, post-purchase voucher, discount applied at checkout without prior announcement); price changes without a reduction announcement.
- A "personal" code given to everyone is an announced reduction ("20% today with code X").
- Successive campaigns: each reduction uses the lowest price of the prior 30 days, including the previous campaign's promo price. A leaked coupon or hidden discount creates a new "lowest price". The progressive-reduction exception within one campaign depends on national transposition.
- At EU level art. 6a applies to **goods**, not to services or digital content; those answer to the UCPD (no misleading price advantage). **Member states may extend the 30-day rule to services and digital content: check national law.** Safe default: whenever you show "X instead of Y", show the 30-day lowest price.

## 2. Misleading price advantage, "free", bundles
- Misleading price advantage is banned for any product (UCPD art. 6(1)(d)): a "was" price nobody paid, an unproven "total value", a false promotion deadline (Annex I no. 7), a permanent promotion presented as temporary.
- "Free" only for what costs nothing beyond the unavoidable cost of responding and collecting (Annex I no. 20). An item always included in the price is not a "free bonus": say "includes".
- "Worth X" next to an item only with proof: the price you really sell it for, or the median of equivalents with source and date.
- Partitioned prices make totals feel smaller and are regulated: always show the total. [M for the psychology]

## 3. Final price, drip pricing, personalised prices
- Consumer price is final, taxes included, and the total appears before the order (CRD arts. 6(1)(e), 8(2)). Costs revealed only at the end (drip pricing) are misleading.
- Price personalised by automated decision must be disclosed (CRD art. 6(1)(ea)). Consequence for tests: simultaneous price variants by profile need disclosure; alternating periods with the same price for everyone do not. Never lower the price only for those who complain.
- Final order button must state the obligation to pay ("order with obligation to pay" or an equivalent formula), otherwise the consumer is not bound (CRD art. 8(2)).

## 4. Withdrawal right, digital content, guarantees
- 14 days to withdraw from a distance contract without reason (CRD art. 9); refund within 14 days by the same payment method (art. 13).
- Digital content with immediate access (art. 16(m)): the right is lost only if, before delivery, the consumer (1) expressly asked to start, (2) acknowledged losing the right, and (3) received confirmation on a durable medium (art. 8(7)). Missing any of the three, they keep 14 days and pay nothing for what they consumed (art. 14(4)(b)). A pre-ticked box is not consent.
- Digital **content** is not a digital **service**: a ready file or access to finished content tends to be content; an ongoing service with the trader's participation (platform, subscription, live class) tends to be a service, where the right is lost only on full performance (art. 16(a)). Mixed products are a grey zone: [LAWYER], and do not build the offer on an optimistic reading.
- A commercial guarantee is what you give beyond the law; it must be honored as advertised, and the legal right must not be presented as a feature of the offer (Annex I no. 10). Anti-abuse rules (one refund per person) must be clear before purchase.
- Withdrawal function: Directive 2023/2673 inserted art. 11a in the CRD: contracts concluded through an online interface must offer a visible withdrawal function during the period, with two-step confirmation and acknowledgement. Member states adopt by 19/12/2025 and apply from 19/06/2026 (Directive text read 15/11/2026); national transposition varies; exceptions to the right still apply.
- Gift codes: the buyer keeps the withdrawal right until delivery starts; treat activation by the recipient as the moment of consent.

## 5. Bumps, upsells and dark patterns
- Additional payments need express consent; a default-ticked box gives a right to refund (CRD art. 22). Applies to bumps, insurance, donations, bundled subscriptions.
- Each additional purchase repeats the first one's obligations: total price, what it is, pay-obligation button, its own withdrawal waiver if it is immediate digital content.
- An offer must be recognisable as an offer: an upsell disguised as a "welcome lesson" or "last signup step" is misleading (UCPD arts. 6-7).
- Declining must be as easy and neutral as accepting: equal visual weight, no guilt line (confirmshaming), one refusal closes the offer for that session, at most one downsell. Repeating the ask is nagging (Guidance 2021/C 526/01, 4.2.7).
- The main purchase never depends on the upsell: access to what was paid comes first.

## 6. Urgency, deadlines, existing customers, gifts
- Deadlines only real, definitive and equal for all; extending one makes it false (Annex I no. 7). Decide the end date before announcing.
- A per-visitor countdown in an evergreen funnel is legitimate only if the condition really ends for that person and does not return by another path; if price varies per person automatically, disclosure applies. When in doubt, do not use it. [F on legitimacy]
- Dated launch price: the cleanest urgency, but it may count as a reduction announcement (section 1); after the increase, any reduction carries the launch price as the 30-day lowest price for a month.
- Promotions to existing customers: genuinely personal reductions sit outside art. 6a at EU level, but a national authority may read a mass app/e-mail offer as a general announcement. Safe position: show the 30-day lowest price.
- Exit-intent or pause coupons teach abandonment, create a new lowest price and discriminate against full-price buyers.
- Seasonal dates (Black Friday, Christmas, back-to-school) are when enforcement looks hardest. Alternatives to price cuts: frame as a gift, a bundle that exists only on that date, or keep the current price and state until when it applies.
- Gifts: written rules before selling (code validity, who may redeem, what if the recipient already owns it, withdrawal before activation), per-account limits on promo prices to avoid resale. A single-purpose voucher (fixed face value, defined product) is taxed for VAT at issue (Directive 2016/1065). [ACCOUNTANT]

## 7. VAT and payment-method notes
- B2C digital sales in the EU use the customer's country VAT (Directive 2006/112 art. 58), declared through the One-Stop Shop. A micro-business established in one member state with up to 10,000 EUR/year of cross-border digital sales may apply its own country's VAT (art. 59c). Above that, margin changes overnight. [ACCOUNTANT]
- "Buy now, pay later" and instalments are consumer credit with their own rules (Directive 2023/2225, applicable from 20/11/2026); only with a provider that carries the information duties; rarely sensible for low ticket.
- One-click upsell exists only with methods that store credentials (card, wallet, mandate). Bank-transfer and instant-payment methods turn "one click" into a new payment: measure take rate per method.

## 8. Enforcement
Enforcement is real and fines are large: national consumer authorities have fined large marketplaces in 2025-2026 for missing or wrong 30-day lowest prices and for computing discounts on a "regular" price. Look up the buyer country's authority and its recent decisions before a campaign.
