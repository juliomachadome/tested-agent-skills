5. Ariely, D. *Predictably Irrational, Revised and Expanded Edition: The Hidden Forces That Shape Our Decisions*. Harper Perennial, 2010 (first ed. 2008). ## Studies

DOI 10.2307/1914185. **P** (identity via Crossref `https://api.crossref.org/works/10.2307/1914185` 15/11/2026: title, both authors, Econometrica 47(2), March 1979, start page 263; full text not read, JSTOR shows a bot challenge). 11. Huber, J., Payne, J., Puto, C. "Adding Asymmetrically Dominated Alternatives: Violations of Regularity and the Similarity Hypothesis". *Journal of Consumer Research* 9(1), 90- (June 1982). DOI 10.1086/208899. **P**: abstract read at the publisher page this pass (a dominated option can raise the share of the option that dominates it); the full text is paywalled and the lab design not read. Supports: decoy plans as a hypothesis to test, not a result to assume.
12. Anderson, E., Simester, D. "Effects of $9 Price Endings on Retail Sales: Evidence from Field Experiments". *Quantitative Marketing and Economics* 1(1), 93-110 (2003). DOI 10.1023/A:1023581927405. **P** 15/11/2026 (Springer abstract read in a browser): three field experiments; a $9 ending increased demand in all three; stronger for new items; some evidence it is less effective with "Sale" cues; suggests it works better when customers have limited information. A catalogue-retailer experiment, not digital goods.
13. Anderson, E., Simester, D. The price-ending and sale-sign claims are not read in this source; the skill takes them from item 12.
