# Diagnosis playbook: symptom, likely causes, check, fix

Use it top-down. Step 0 first: a wrong denominator, an unclear event or a missing cohort makes every branch meaningless. Thresholds are **[F]** (practitioner starting points) unless tagged; the team's own last 8-12 weeks replace them. Evidence tags: **[C]** consensus, **[M]** serious but single or correlational, **[F]** practitioner or vendor, **[X]** myth. Nothing is copied from a source; ideas are applied. Funnel, cohort and experiment mechanics live in `skills/growth-analytics`; screen and flow fixes live in `skills/ux-design`. Here we decide what to build, stop or learn.

## Step 0: is the data trustworthy?
| Check | How | If it fails |
|---|---|---|
| Event definitions | Is "used the feature" one named event with one meaning, fired on success, not on page view? | Write the definition next to the metric; do not read adoption until it exists |
| Denominator | Adoption = users who did X / users who could have done X (eligible, on the right plan and platform), not / all accounts | Recompute and label both |
| Cohorts | Weekly signup cohorts, same segment, same period; no blending of old and new users | Split before concluding |
| Volume | Under about 30 users in a cell, a rate moves 10+ points on noise (95% range at n=30, 50%: roughly +-18 points) | Write "not enough data"; talk to the users instead |
| Change calendar | Releases, pricing, packaging, campaigns, outages, tracking changes in the window | Line them up on a timeline before naming a cause |
| Instrumentation date | When did the event start firing? | Only compare periods after that date |

## Core arithmetic
- **RICE** = reach x impact x confidence / effort. Reach in people or events per period; impact 3 massive, 2 high, 1 medium, 0.5 low, 0.25 minimal; confidence 100, 80, 50 percent; effort in person-months **[F]** (Intercom scale). Worked: A = 2,000 x 2 x 0.8 / 2 = 1,600; B = 500 x 3 x 0.5 / 1 = 750; C = 8,000 x 0.5 x 1.0 / 4 = 1,000. Order A, C, B. B has the highest impact but half the confidence; one week of discovery on B may raise its confidence to 80 percent (score 1,200) and is cheaper than building it.
- **Adoption funnel**: aware -> tried -> repeated. Example, 10,000 eligible accounts: 6,200 saw the entry point (aware 62%); 1,240 tried (20% of aware, 12.4% of eligible); 310 repeated within 14 days (25% of tried, 3.1% of eligible). The worst step is "aware to tried" (20%) in absolute reach lost, but "tried to repeated" (25%) says the value is not delivered once tried. Fix order: if repeat is weak, fix value before discoverability; promoting a feature people try once and drop only burns trust.
- **Retention reading**: weekly cohort, share still active. Curve A: 100, 40, 28, 23, 21, 21, 21 (flattens near 21%: a core exists, grow acquisition and the core). Curve B: 100, 40, 25, 18, 12, 8, 5 (never flattens: leaky product, acquisition makes it worse). A flat curve at a low level (5%) is a niche or a weak job. Compare cohorts by signup month: if newer cohorts sit lower, something changed in acquisition or onboarding.
- **Opportunity sizing** (assumption chain, each link a test): 40,000 monthly active users x 15% have the problem (survey or events: 6,000) x 30% would act (fake door: 1,800) x 96 per year (8 per month) = 172,800 per year at full conversion. Show the range with low and high for each link; if the low case does not cover the effort, stop.
- **Backlog arithmetic**: 400 items at 12 shipped per quarter is 33 quarters of work. A backlog is a queue; if it is longer than about two quarters of capacity, items beyond that are a wish list, not a plan.
- **Price change and churn**: monthly churn 3% gives expected lifetime 1/0.03 = 33 months. A 20% price rise with churn at 4.5% gives 22 months. Revenue per account over lifetime: 1.0 x 33 = 33 versus 1.2 x 22 = 26.4. The rise lost about 20% of lifetime value unless churn falls back; compare by plan and by signup date before blaming the price.

## Symptom table
| Symptom | Likely causes | How to check | Fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Feature shipped, nobody uses it** | not found (awareness); found but not understood; understood but not valuable; wrong segment or platform; value arrives too late; built for an assumed need | Funnel aware -> tried -> repeated on eligible users; watch 5 sessions of people who saw it and did not try; interview 5 who tried once and dropped; compare with the evidence in the original PRD | Repeat weak: reopen discovery on the job, do not promote. Tried weak: copy, placement, first-use path (with ux-design). Aware weak: in-context entry point at the moment of need. If still low after one fix cycle with a stated threshold, remove it **[M]** |
| **Roadmap is a feature list with dates** | outcomes absent; dates promised without discovery; sales or leadership dictate items; delivery treated as success | For each line: which outcome, which evidence, who owns, what stops it? Count lines that fail | Rewrite as Now / Next / Later with outcome, evidence, owner, stop condition (`playbooks.md` 5); dates only for external commitments, labelled |
| **Backlog of 400 items** | no outcome to filter by; nothing ever deleted; every request logged as a promise | Age distribution; share older than 6 months; share with an evidence link; capacity in items per quarter | Declare bankruptcy on items older than 6 months with no owner and no evidence (archive, do not agonise); keep 2 quarters of capacity as ready; publish a "not doing" list with reasons |
| **Stakeholders override priorities (HiPPO)** | no shared outcome or criteria; scores hidden; decisions undocumented | List last 5 overrides: who, what, what evidence, what happened after | Agree outcome and criteria beforehand; show the scoring and confidence in the open; offer a cheap test instead of a fight; log the decision with a review date (`playbooks.md` 8). Leader asks are inputs with a hypothesis to test, not orders to skip the reason test |
| **Team ships a lot, metrics do not move** | output measured instead of outcome; features not tied to a driver; effect hidden by seasonality or mix; shipped features unused | Map every shipped item of the last quarter to a metric and an expected effect; read before-and-after with a control or cohort; adoption of each item | Set a target outcome per team; stop counting shipped items; run the 30/60/90 review (`playbooks.md` 6); kill or scale rule decided before launch **[M]** (output vs outcome, Perri) |
| **Discovery skipped, "users asked for it"** | request equals solution; vocal minority; one big customer; no job understood | Ask: what were they trying to do? last time it happened? what did they do instead? how many others? | Turn the request into the underlying job; 5-8 interviews in the segment; test the riskiest assumption with a fake door before building (`playbooks.md` 1-2); build only what the evidence supports |
| **Customers say they love it, but nobody pays, commits or returns** | notes full of compliments, generic habits ("I usually") and "I would buy"; the idea was pitched early, so people protected the founder; no ask for time, reputation or money; leads with no dated next step; segment so broad that feedback contradicts itself | Re-read 5 interview notes and tag each line: fact about the past, compliment, hypothetical or feature request. Count commitments by currency (time, reputation, money). List leads with no dated next step | Rerun with the rules in `discovery-and-jtbd.md` (their life, specific past events, no pitch before the story, one question that could kill the idea); end each conversation with a concrete ask (a trial, a meeting with the boss, a deposit, a pilot price); narrow the segment until answers agree. Compliments and "keep me posted" are polite exits, not demand (Fitzpatrick, ch. 2, 5) **[M]** |
| **"We have product-market fit" but nobody shows a cohort table** | paying customers read as proof; story sells, product does not hold; early adopters read as the mass market; vanity totals (signups, downloads, app score) | Conversion (new users who reach the core value) and retention by signup cohort; B2B: renewals and expansion by customer; where users drop between "understand", "reach value" and "decide it is worth it"; ask someone outside the team to read the table | Measure first, claim later; talk to the people who dropped (they know the barrier, retained users do not); fix the largest barrier, measure again; do not scale spend before a curve flattens (Levine ch. 8; Ries ch. 7 on cohort and vanity metrics) **[F]** |
| **Activation is low** | users never reach the first value moment; setup too long; wrong users acquired; value moment undefined | Define the value event; funnel signup -> setup -> first value, time to value, by source; watch drop-offs | Fix the step with the biggest drop; remove fields; pre-fill; segment by source; if one source activates 3x better, shift acquisition. Deep work: onboarding in `skills/ux-design` and `skills/growth-analytics` |
| **Retention curve does not flatten** | no recurring need; value not repeated; no habit trigger; poor fit; early churners acquired by promos | Cohort curves by segment, source, first-week behaviour; find the action in week 1 that separates retained from lost | Strengthen the repeated job, not notifications; narrow to the segment whose curve flattens; check the 40 percent "very disappointed" survey (Ellis; his own blog calls the threshold "admittedly a bit arbitrary", set after comparing nearly 100 startups; practitioner **[F]**) on the retained segment only |
| **Churn rising after a pricing or packaging change** | price above perceived value; feature moved behind a paywall that people used; grandfathering unclear; communication poor; mix shift in who signs up | Churn by plan, by signup date (before vs after), by feature usage of the moved feature; exit reasons; support tickets; the arithmetic above | Compare cohorts first. If a used feature was moved, restore or offer a bridge; test the packaging on new customers before existing ones. Price design: `skills/pricing`, `skills/offers` |
| **Sales-driven one-off features** | deals closed with promises; no rule for custom work; roadmap by loudest deal | List custom items: revenue, number of customers asking, maintenance cost, fit to ICP | Count requests across accounts; build only if 3+ similar and in ICP; otherwise decline, price as services, or give integrations; give sales a pre-agreed "can say yes / must ask" list |
| **Scope creep and late releases** | scope not bounded; appetite not set; unknowns not de-risked; estimates by one person; dependencies hidden | Compare original acceptance criteria with the current list; slip history; list of unknowns at kickoff; items added after kickoff | Set a time box first and cut scope to fit; split into thin end-to-end slices; add a risk spike for unknowns; every addition removes something or moves the date, said out loud |
| **PRD nobody reads** | too long; written after decisions; no problem or metric; no engineer in the writing | Ask 3 team members to state the problem and success metric without opening it | One page that opens with the problem and the metric; write it with an engineer and a designer; read it aloud in kickoff; keep the template in `prd-template.md` and the guide in `playbooks.md` 3 |
| **AI feature: great demo, poor real quality** | demo on cherry-picked inputs; no labelled test set; real data differs from the demo; no error cost analysis; metric is usage not correctness | Build a labelled set of 100+ real cases; measure the acceptance bar; compare demo inputs vs real distribution | Write the bar as numbers before building (`ai-features.md`); shadow mode first; narrow the scope to where the bar is met; show uncertainty and let people correct. Eval method belongs to llm-engineering |

## Reading across rows
Two or three symptoms together usually share a root: unclear outcome, missing discovery, no stop condition. State the single most likely cause, a second one, the cheapest test for each, and what you could not verify. Do not give a verdict on cause from one cohort or one week of data.
